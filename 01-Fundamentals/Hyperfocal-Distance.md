# Hyperfocal Distance

> [!summary] In one line
> The hyperfocal distance is the focus distance that makes everything from about half
> that distance to infinity look sharp. It gives you the most depth of field an aperture
> can offer when a scene runs to the horizon.

## What it is

Focus on the horizon and part of your depth of field is wasted: there is nothing beyond
infinity to make sharp. Bring the focus closer and the near limit comes closer too, while
infinity stays sharp. The closest focus distance that still keeps infinity sharp is the
**hyperfocal distance**.

Focus there, and everything from about half that distance to infinity is acceptably
sharp. No other focus distance gives you more depth with that lens and aperture.

Example: on the L10 at 24 mm and f/5.6, the hyperfocal distance is about 1.5 m. Focus at
1.5 m and everything from about 0.8 m to the horizon looks sharp.

## How it works

The hyperfocal distance comes from the same things that set [[Depth-of-Field]]: focal
length, aperture, sensor size and the circle of confusion (the largest blur that still
looks sharp).

| Change | What happens to the hyperfocal distance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Longer focal length | Much further: it grows with the square of the focal length | L10 at f/8: about 1.1 m at 24 mm, about 10 m at 75 mm |
| Close the aperture two stops | Half as far | L10 at 24 mm: about 2.1 m at f/4, about 1.1 m at f/8 |
| Smaller sensor, same view and f-number | Closer, by about the crop factor | 24 mm at f/2.8: about 7 m on full frame, 3 m on the L10 |
| Stricter idea of "sharp" | Further | Halve the blur you accept and it doubles |

From 24 mm to 75 mm is about three times the focal length, so the distance grows about
nine to ten times.

### Why the near limit is half the distance

Blur doesn't grow evenly with distance. Lens maths works with 1 ÷ distance. Focused at
2 m (1 ÷ 2 = 0.5), a point at 1 m (1 ÷ 1 = 1) and a point at infinity (1 ÷ ∞ = 0) are
both 0.5 away, so they are equally blurred. That holds at any focus distance: a point
at half the distance blurs exactly as much as infinity.

At the hyperfocal distance, that blur is just small enough to pass as sharp. So the zone
runs from half the distance all the way out.

The same fact gives you the most useful rule in this note. **Focus at twice the distance
of the nearest thing that must be sharp**, and that thing and the horizon end up equally
sharp. The aperture then decides whether "equally sharp" is sharp enough.

### Why "focus one third into the scene" is unreliable

The best focus point sits exactly one third of the way in only when the far end is
twice as far as the near end, such as a table-top from 1 m to 2 m. For deeper scenes it
is closer than a third. For a scene that runs to the horizon, "one third" has no
meaning, since a third of infinity is still infinity. People guess, usually too far.

Example: a railing 1 m away and buildings 30 m away, L10 at 24 mm, f/5.6. Focus "a
third of the way in", at about 10 m, and the sharp zone starts at about 1.3 m: the
railing is soft. Focus at 2 m instead and everything from about 0.9 m to infinity is
sharp.

## How to use it

### Method 1: a short table for the L10 at 24 mm

| Aperture | Focus at (hyperfocal distance) | Sharp from about |
|---|---|---|
| f/1.7 | 5.0 m | 2.5 m to infinity |
| f/2.8 | 3.0 m | 1.5 m to infinity |
| f/4 | 2.1 m | 1.1 m to infinity |
| f/5.6 | 1.5 m | 0.8 m to infinity |
| f/8 | 1.1 m | 0.5 m to infinity |

These values are approximate. They use the usual circle of confusion for the L10's sensor
(about 0.014 mm), which suits a print about A4 size or a phone screen. At 75 mm, every
distance is about ten times longer. Stop at f/8: from about f/5.6–f/8, diffraction
softens the whole photo on this sensor. → [[Aperture]]

### Method 2: an app

A depth-of-field calculator does the sums for any focal length, aperture and distance.
PhotoPills, already listed in [[Resources]], has one. If the app doesn't list the L10,
pick any full-frame camera, enter the equivalent focal length (24–75 mm) and multiply the
f-number by 2.2: for f/4 on the L10, enter about f/9. Because the crop factor is about
2.2, this gives about the same answer.

### Method 3: twice the nearest distance

1. Find the nearest thing that must be sharp. Estimate its distance (one adult step is
   about 0.7 m).
2. Focus at twice that distance.
3. Choose an aperture whose hyperfocal distance (from the table) is no further than
   your focus distance.

A rock 1 m away: focus at 2 m. f/5.6 (1.5 m) works; f/4 (2.1 m) falls a little short.

### Zone focusing for street

Zone focusing means setting the focus and aperture in advance, so a band of distances is
sharp, then shooting without waiting for autofocus. A good street setting on the L10:
24 mm, f/5.6, focus at about 2.5 m, which keeps about 1 m to infinity sharp. In dimmer
light, f/4 focused at 2 m covers about 1 m to 35 m.

To set it, use [[Back-Button-Focus]]: press [AF ON] once on something at the right
distance, then leave it alone. Or set the distance in manual focus.
→ [[Street-Photography]]

### Why 100% view makes "acceptably sharp" look soft

The tables assume a modest viewing size. On the L10, with pixels about 3 µm wide, the
0.014 mm blur they accept is four to five pixels wide. At 100% on a monitor you see every
pixel, so that blur is easy to spot. The horizon looks soft, though the table said sharp.

If you will print big or crop hard, use a stricter standard. Focus at twice the table's
distance: infinity gets twice as sharp, and the near limit moves out to about the
table's focus distance.

## On your kit

- **L10:** the only device where you choose the aperture, so hyperfocal focusing is a
  real decision here. Work at 24 mm and f/5.6, or f/8 at most.
- **iPhone 15 Pro Max:** fixed apertures and small sensors keep the hyperfocal distance
  short. For a landscape, press and hold on something at about twice the distance of
  the nearest important thing. That locks focus and exposure there (AE/AF LOCK); drag
  the sun icon if the brightness needs fixing.
- **Pocket 3:** a wide 20 mm on a small sensor gives deep focus already. If something
  close and something far must both be sharp, step back from the close one.
- **Action 5 Pro:** a fixed-focus lens is the hyperfocal idea built in. The focus is set
  so that everything from about 35 cm to infinity is sharp, and you can't change it.

## Common mistakes

- **Focusing on infinity to get the mountains sharp.** Part of the depth of field is
  wasted beyond the horizon, and the foreground goes soft. Focus at the hyperfocal
  distance.
- **Using "one third into the scene".** It only works when the far end is about twice as
  far as the near end. Focus at twice the nearest distance instead.
- **Using the 24 mm numbers after zooming.** At 75 mm the hyperfocal distance is about
  ten times further. Check the focal length first.
- **Stopping down to f/16 for more depth.** On the L10 you lose more detail to
  diffraction than you gain. Focus in the right place at f/5.6–f/8.
- **Trusting the table for a big print.** It assumes a modest viewing size. For big
  prints or crops, focus at twice the table's distance.

## Try it

Twenty minutes, on the L10 at 24 mm, in aperture priority (A) mode with manual focus.
Find a scene with something about 1 m away (a railing, a plant) and something far away.

1. At f/5.6, focus on the near object. Shoot.
2. Focus on the far object. Shoot.
3. Focus on something about 2 m away (about three steps). Shoot.
4. Repeat step 3 at f/2.8.
5. Compare near and far in each frame, first at phone-screen size, then at 100%.

Before you look, write down what you expect to be sharp. This is a small version of
**A1.6**.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- On the L10 at 24 mm and f/4, the hyperfocal distance is about 2.1 m. What is sharp if you focus there, and if you focus at infinity?
> Focused at 2.1 m: about 1.1 m to infinity. Focused at infinity: only about 2.1 m to
> infinity. You lose everything from 1.1 m to 2.1 m, because part of the sharp zone now
> falls beyond the horizon.

> [!question]- Why does zooming from 24 mm to 75 mm push the hyperfocal distance about ten times further?
> It grows with the square of the focal length. 75 mm is about three times 24 mm, and
> three squared is nine, so the distance grows about nine to ten times.

> [!question]- A rock is 1.5 m away and mountains fill the horizon. Where do you focus, and why not "one third into the scene"?
> At about 3 m, twice the rock's distance. The rock and the mountains are then equally
> sharp, and f/4 or f/5.6 makes that sharp enough. "One third" of a scene that runs to
> infinity has no meaning, and guesses usually land too far.

> [!question]- The table says the far hills will be sharp, but at 100% they look soft. What went wrong?
> Nothing in the camera. The table accepts a blur of four to five pixels on the L10,
> and 100% view shows every pixel. For big prints or crops, focus at twice the table's
> distance.

> [!question]- Why does the Action 5 Pro never need to focus?
> Its very wide lens and small real opening make the hyperfocal distance short. The
> maker fixed the focus so that everything from about 35 cm to infinity is sharp.
