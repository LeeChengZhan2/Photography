# Long Exposure

> [!summary] In one line
> A long exposure keeps the shutter open from about 1/15 s to several minutes, so
> movement turns into silky water, light trails and streaking clouds. Anything still
> stays sharp, as long as the camera doesn't move.

## What it is

At normal shutter speeds you freeze a moment. A long exposure records a stretch of time
in one frame. Moving things smear or vanish; still things stay sharp. The contrast
between the two is the picture. → [[Shutter-Speed]]

| Subject | Starting shutter speed | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| A tap, fountain or waterfall | 1/4–2 s | Silky water |
| Waves on a beach | 1–30 s | A soft, misty sea |
| Car lights at night | 5–30 s | Light trails |
| Moving clouds | 30 s to several minutes | Streaks across the sky |
| A busy square, emptied of people | Several minutes | Walkers vanish; anyone standing still leaves a ghost |
| Star trails | Many minutes to hours, usually as many shorter frames | Arcs and streaks of stars |

Star trails need a dark sky, which Singapore's light pollution makes hard to find.
→ [[Night-and-Astrophotography]]

## How it works

### Keep the camera still

The camera must not move during the whole exposure. At these speeds, hand-holding gets
unreliable, even with stabilisation. You need:

- **A support.** A tripod is best. A wall, a railing, a bench or a beanbag (a bag of
  rice works) is free. Wedge the camera so it can't tip or slide.
- **A hands-off release.** Pressing the button shakes the camera. Use the self-timer on
  its shortest delay, or a remote.
- **Solid ground.** Footbridges shake when people walk or traffic passes. Wait for a
  quiet moment, or shoot from the ground.

Some stabilisation systems can add blur when the camera sits on a tripod. If your
tripod shots come out soft, check the manual about switching stabilisation off.

### Get enough time in daylight

Daylight is too bright. Even at f/16 and ISO 100, bright sun calls for about 1/125 s.
Reaching 1 s needs about 7 stops less light. Three ways to get there:

1. **Wait for dusk.** Light falls fast after sunset, which is around 19:00 all year in
   Singapore. Blue hour (the deep-blue twilight after sunset) and night give exposures
   of a second or more with no filter.
2. **Use an ND filter.** A neutral density (ND) filter is dark glass in front of the
   lens. It cuts light without changing colour. → [[Lens-Filters]]
3. **Stack short frames.** Shoot 20–50 normal frames from a support and average them in
   editing software. Water and clouds smooth out much like a long exposure. A "median"
   blend keeps, for each pixel, the value most frames agree on, so passing people
   disappear. It's free.

| ND filter | Stops | 1/125 s becomes |
|---|---|---|
| ND8 | 3 | 1/15 s |
| ND64 | 6 | 1/2 s |
| ND1000 | 10 | 8 s |

### Bulb mode

Most cameras' timed speeds stop at 30 or 60 seconds. For longer, **Bulb** (B) keeps the
shutter open for as long as you hold the button, or until you press a remote again.
Time it with your phone's stopwatch.

### Noise in long exposures

During a long exposure the sensor slowly builds up a weak false signal from heat. You
see it as **hot pixels** (bright coloured dots) and extra noise. It gets worse with
longer exposures and higher temperatures, and Singapore is warm.

Many cameras offer **long-exposure noise reduction**. After your exposure, the camera
takes a second one of the same length with the shutter closed. That "dark frame" holds
only the hot pixels, so the camera subtracts it from your photo. It works, but it
doubles the wait: a 30 s exposure takes a minute. Turn it off for star trails and
stacks, where the gaps would break the sequence. → [[Noise-Reduction]]

## How to use it

A starting routine for light trails at night:

1. Find solid ground above or beside a busy road.
2. Put the camera on a support and set the self-timer.
3. Use M mode, ISO 100, f/8 to f/11. On the L10, f/11 is a little soft from
   diffraction (→ [[Aperture]]), a fair price for longer trails.
4. Focus on the road with autofocus, then switch to manual focus so it can't hunt
   mid-exposure. → [[Manual-Focus]]
5. Start at 10 s. Begin the exposure just before a stream of cars arrives.
6. Check the [[Histogram]]. Too bright? Close the aperture or lower the ISO, and keep
   the time: the time sets the length of the trails.

### Two creative techniques

**Intentional camera movement (ICM):** move the camera on purpose while the shutter is
open. Sweep it upwards past tall tree trunks, or sideways past a row of lit shopfronts.
Shapes smear into soft strokes of colour, like a painting. Try about 1/8–1 s. In bright
sun that needs an ND filter; in shade or at dusk a small aperture may be enough. Start
moving before you press the button, move smoothly, and shoot many frames: few will work.

**Multiple exposure:** two or more frames laid over each other in one picture, such as
a face and the leaves of a tree. Some cameras combine frames in camera, and the L10 has
a [Multiple Exposure] setting. Or shoot the frames normally and combine them
in an editor with layers: put one above the other and use a "Lighten" or "Screen"
blend, or lower the top layer's opacity. The other frame shows through the dark areas.

## On your kit

- **Lumix L10:** your long-exposure camera. The shutter goes to 60 s, and its
  [T] (Time) setting also stops at about 60 s: there's no Bulb. **Live View Composite**
  adds only *new* bright areas during a long exposure, for up to 3 hours, so light
  trails build up without the rest of the scene blowing out. It's a ready-made
  "abstract" frame for A1.2. There's no built-in ND, so daylight means a filter or dusk.
- **Action 5 Pro:** PRO mode photos go to 30 s, the longest after the L10. Stills get
  no stabilisation, so mount or rest it. It's waterproof, so it can sit at the water's
  edge for waves.
- **Pocket 3:** Pro mode stops at 1 s. That's enough for A1.2's slow end at dusk, and
  for short light trails.
- **iPhone 15 Pro Max:** manual shutter in Halide or Lightroom stops at about 1 s.
  **Night mode** switches on by itself in dim light. It records several frames over a
  second or more and merges them into a 12 MP photo. It's built to make a dark scene
  sharp and bright, not to blur motion. For blur, use the **Live Photo long-exposure
  effect**: take a Live Photo, then choose the Long Exposure effect in the Photos app.
  It blends the short clip a Live Photo records (about 3 seconds) into one frame. Hold
  the phone very still, or rest it on something.

## Common mistakes

- **Pressing the button with your finger.** The press shakes the camera. Use the
  self-timer or a remote.
- **Leaving autofocus on.** It can hunt in the dark and refocus between frames. Focus
  once, then switch to manual.
- **Shortening the time when the frame is too bright.** That shortens the trails and
  roughens the water. Close the aperture or lower the ISO first.
- **Trusting a busy footbridge.** Footsteps and traffic make it vibrate. Shoot from
  solid ground, or between passing loads.
- **Thinking the camera has frozen.** With long-exposure noise reduction on, it's
  taking the dark frame. Wait, or turn it off for sequences.

## Try it

Twenty minutes, at dusk or in a dim room, with a running tap or a fountain:

1. Put the L10 on a support and set the self-timer.
2. In S mode at ISO 100, shoot at 1/15 s, 1/4 s, 1 s and, if it's dark enough, 4 s.
3. Compare the water. Where does it change from "moving" to "silky"?
4. After dark, try Live View Composite on a road for light trails.

This covers the slow end of **A1.2**.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- At f/16 and ISO 100 in sun, the meter says 1/125 s. You want 1 s. How strong an ND filter do you need?
> 1/125 s to 1 s is 7 stops (1/60, 1/30, 1/15, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1). So you need about a
> 7-stop ND filter. A 6-stop one gives 1/2 s; a 10-stop one, about 8 s.

> [!question]- Your light-trail frame at f/8 and 20 s is too bright. What do you change first, and why?
> Close the aperture to f/11 or f/16, or lower the ISO if you can. Shortening the time
> would also shorten the trails, and the trails are the picture.

> [!question]- Why does long-exposure noise reduction double the time each shot takes?
> After your exposure the camera takes a dark frame of the same length, with the
> shutter closed, to map the hot pixels. Then it subtracts it from your photo.

> [!question]- Why won't the iPhone's Night mode give you light trails like Live View Composite does?
> Night mode aligns and merges frames to make a sharp, bright photo, so it removes blur
> rather than keeping it. Live View Composite keeps adding new light over the whole
> exposure.

> [!question]- The Action 5 Pro has stabilisation. Why mount it for a 10 s photo?
> Its stabilisation works only for video. Stills get none, so any movement during the
> 10 s shows as blur.
