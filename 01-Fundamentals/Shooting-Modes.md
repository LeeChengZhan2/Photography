# Shooting Modes

> [!summary] In one line
> A shooting mode decides which exposure settings you choose and which the camera
> chooses for you. Pick the mode that gives you control of what matters in this
> picture.

## What it is

Each mode splits the [[Exposure-Triangle|exposure settings]] between you and the camera:

| Mode | You set | The camera sets |
|---|---|---|
| Auto | Almost nothing | Everything: aperture, shutter, ISO, often white balance and flash |
| P (Program) | ISO or Auto ISO, exposure compensation | Aperture and shutter speed |
| A or Av (Aperture priority) | Aperture, ISO | Shutter speed |
| S or Tv (Shutter priority) | Shutter speed, ISO | Aperture |
| M (Manual) | Aperture, shutter speed, ISO | Nothing; the meter only advises |
| M with Auto ISO | Aperture, shutter speed | ISO |

Av and Tv are Canon's names ("aperture value", "time value"). Panasonic, Nikon and Sony
use A and S. **Auto ISO**, where the camera picks the ISO, can also be used in P, A and
S.

## How it works

In P, A and S, the camera meters the scene and fills in whatever you left free, so the
photo comes out at the meter's brightness. You steer it with
[[Exposure-Compensation]].

Each mode fails in its own way when the camera runs out of room:

- **A:** the shutter slows as the light falls. In a dim room at f/4 and ISO 400 it may
  pick 1/15 s, and people blur. Auto ISO with a minimum shutter speed prevents this.
- **S:** the aperture can only open so far. Indoors at 1/1000 s with a fixed ISO, the
  L10 reaches f/1.7 (f/2.8 at 75 mm) and the photo still comes out dark.
- **P:** you don't know which pair it picked unless you look. Most cameras let you
  "shift" to another pair with the same brightness.
- **M:** nothing changes when the light does. Walk from shade into sun, and every photo
  is overexposed until you adjust.

**M with Auto ISO** is a hybrid. You fix the two settings that shape the picture,
aperture for depth and shutter for motion, and the camera floats the ISO to hold the
brightness. Exposure compensation then moves the ISO.

## How to use it

| Situation | Mode | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Most stills: walking around, travel, portraits | A, with Auto ISO and a minimum shutter speed | Depth of field is usually the main choice |
| Moving subjects: sport, children, traffic, panning | S, or M with Auto ISO | Shutter speed decides sharp or blurred |
| Steady light: a room, a stage, a night scene on a support | M | The exposure stays the same from frame to frame |
| Flash | M | You set the balance of flash and room light yourself |
| Panoramas and brackets | M | Every frame must match before merging (→ [[Photo-Merges]]) |
| Video | M | Brightness shouldn't jump mid-shot, and the frame rate sets the shutter (→ [[Shutter-Angle]]) |
| Handing the camera to a friend | P or Auto | Quick and safe |

**See your exposure before you shoot.** In M, some cameras keep the screen and
viewfinder bright so you can compose, instead of showing the exposure you have set. A
frame that looks fine on screen can then come out dark. Panasonic cameras usually have
a setting that shows the real exposure in M (often called Constant Preview); check the
L10's manual and turn it on, especially for A1.5. Flash is the exception: the screen
can't show the flash, so a brightened view helps there.

### What experienced photographers use

There's no "professional" mode. Many experienced photographers spend most of their
time in **A with Auto ISO**: it's quick, and depth of field is usually what they care
about. Sports and event shooters often use **M with Auto ISO**: shutter fixed for the
action, aperture wide open, ISO floating. Studio, flash and landscape photographers use
**M**, because their light is steady or under their control.

What they share is that they always know what the camera is deciding. That's the real
skill. It's also why the syllabus puts manual first: learn what the camera decides
before you let it decide again.

### Phones and the DJIs

With a fixed aperture there's no aperture priority, and the modes collapse:

| Device and mode | Works like |
|---|---|
| iPhone, Apple's Camera app | P with exposure compensation: the phone picks shutter and ISO |
| Halide or Lightroom, shutter set, ISO left on auto (if the app allows) | S |
| Halide or Lightroom, shutter and ISO both set | M |
| Pocket 3 Pro mode, Action 5 Pro PRO mode | M: you set shutter and ISO |

## On your kit

- **Lumix L10:** P, A, S and M, with an aperture ring for the aperture. It's the only
  device where aperture priority means anything. Note your default mode and Auto ISO
  ceiling in [[My-Kit]] once A1.3 gives you a number.
- **iPhone 15 Pro Max:** Apple's Camera app is always automatic, steered by AE/AF lock
  and exposure compensation. For manual shutter and ISO, use Halide or Lightroom.
- **Pocket 3 and Action 5 Pro:** an automatic mode, or Pro / PRO mode, which works like
  M.

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking M is "more professional".** It's one tool. Use whichever mode controls
  what matters in this shot.
- **A mode in dim light without a minimum shutter.** The camera picks 1/15 s and
  anything moving blurs. Set Auto ISO's minimum shutter speed.
- **Forgetting that M ignores changing light.** Re-check the exposure whenever you move
  between sun and shade.
- **S mode with a fixed ISO in dim light.** The aperture runs out and the photos come
  out dark. Turn on Auto ISO, or watch for the camera's warning.
- **Never leaving Auto.** You learn nothing about what the camera decided. Use P at
  least, and read the settings it picked.

## Try it

Fifteen minutes on the L10, at one scene with something moving through it:

1. In A at f/2.8, shoot and note the shutter speed the camera picked.
2. In S at 1/500 s, shoot and note the aperture and ISO it picked.
3. In M, copy the settings from step 1. Shoot, then walk into shade or sun and shoot
   again without changing anything.
4. For each frame, write one line: what did I decide, and what did the camera decide?

This prepares you for **A1.5**, which is all manual.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- In A mode at f/2 indoors, your photos of a dancing child are blurred. What happened, and what's the fix?
> The camera chose a slow shutter to get enough light. Set Auto ISO with a minimum
> shutter of about 1/500 s, or use S or M with Auto ISO at 1/500 s.

> [!question]- Why shoot a panorama in M rather than A?
> In A, each frame is metered separately, so a frame with more sky comes out darker.
> In M every frame matches, so they merge without visible steps.

> [!question]- In M with Auto ISO you add −1 EV. Which setting changes, and how?
> The ISO, which halves: for example from ISO 1600 to 800. Your aperture and shutter
> stay where you put them.

> [!question]- On the Pocket 3, what is Pro mode equivalent to, and why is there no aperture priority?
> Manual: you set shutter and ISO. The aperture is fixed at f/2.0, so there's nothing
> for aperture priority to control.

> [!question]- Why do many sports shooters prefer M with Auto ISO to S?
> They can fix the shutter for the action and keep the aperture wide open for the most
> light. Only the ISO floats, so nothing they care about changes behind their back.
