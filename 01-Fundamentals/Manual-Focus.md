# Manual Focus

> [!summary] In one line
> In manual focus you set the focus distance yourself. You need it exactly where
> autofocus fails: in the dark, on plain surfaces, through fences, up close, on stars
> and in some video shots.

## What it is

In manual focus (MF), the camera stops choosing the focus distance. You turn a ring or
dial, watch the picture, and stop when the right part is sharp. The camera still helps,
with focus aids that show you what is sharp.

Manual focus isn't old-fashioned. It is the tool for the situations autofocus gets
wrong, and a way to set focus once and keep it there.

## How it works

### Why autofocus fails where you don't

Autofocus needs light and contrast at the AF point, and it has to guess which thing you
mean. You can see the subject and decide; the camera can only measure.
→ [[Autofocus-Modes]]

| Situation | What goes wrong with AF | What to do in MF |
|---|---|---|
| Low light | Too little light to measure, so it hunts | Magnify a bright edge or a lamp at the same distance |
| Low contrast: a wall, sky, fog | Nothing for it to grip | Focus on any edge at the same distance |
| Through glass or a fence | It focuses on the glass or the wire | Put the lens close to it and focus past it |
| Macro | The sharp zone is millimetres deep, and AF picks the wrong part | Set focus roughly, then move the camera slowly forward and back |
| Stars | The points are too small and faint | Magnify a bright star and make it as small as possible |
| Video | AF can hunt or jump in the middle of a shot | Set focus and leave it, or move it on purpose |

Why the fence trick works: when the wire is very close to the lens and you focus far
beyond it, the wire is so far outside the [[Depth-of-Field|depth of field]] that its
blur spreads out and almost disappears. A wide aperture makes it vanish even more.

### Focus aids

- **Magnification:** the camera enlarges part of the picture on the screen or in the
  viewfinder. At normal size, a 3-inch screen makes almost everything look sharp.
  Magnified, you can see the difference.
- **Focus peaking:** the camera paints a coloured outline on edges it sees as sharp. It
  is quick for a rough check. But it only looks for strong edges, so a range of
  distances can glow at once. Use magnification for the final check.

Check the L10's manual for both, and for how to switch them on in MF.

### AF with manual override

Many cameras let you focus with AF, then fine-tune by hand without changing modes. This
is often called AF+MF. It is handy for macro and for shooting through things. Check
whether the L10 offers it.

### Pre-focusing and zone focusing

**Pre-focusing** means focusing on a spot before the subject gets there: a doorway, the
bottom of a slide, a bird's favourite perch. When the moment comes, the camera doesn't
need to focus at all.

**Zone focusing** goes further. You choose a distance and an aperture so that a whole
band of distances is sharp, then shoot without focusing. → [[Hyperfocal-Distance]]

### Focus breathing

Some lenses change their angle of view slightly as you focus, so in video the frame
seems to grow or shrink during a focus change.

## How to use it

**A still subject:**

1. Switch to MF.
2. Magnify the part that must be sharp: the nearest eye, the text on a label.
3. Turn the focus past sharp, then back, and stop at the sharpest point. You only know
   where the peak is once sharpness starts to drop again.
4. Shoot, then check the frame zoomed in.

**Stars:** don't trust the end of the focus range. Many modern lenses can focus past
infinity, so the far end may be soft. Magnify a bright star or a distant light, and
turn until it is the smallest, sharpest point. Shoot a test, zoom in, adjust, then
leave the focus alone. → [[Night-and-Astrophotography]]

**Macro:** set the focus roughly, then rock your body slowly forward and back until the
right part snaps sharp. At a few centimetres, moving the camera is a finer control than
turning the focus.

**Video:** set the focus before you press record, so the shot doesn't drift. To move
focus from one subject to another (a "focus pull"), rehearse the turn first, then do it
smoothly.

## On your kit

- **L10:** normal AF works from 50 cm. In AF Macro and MF it focuses down to 3 cm at
  24 mm, and 30 cm at 75 mm. Check the manual for where MF is set, which control turns
  the focus, and how to turn on magnification and focus peaking. The EVF makes it
  easier to judge focus than the rear screen in bright sun.
- **iPhone 15 Pro Max:** Apple's Camera app has no manual focus. The nearest thing is
  press and hold for AE/AF LOCK. Apps such as Halide (stills) and Blackmagic Camera
  (video) add manual focus, usually with an on-screen aid such as peaking.
- **Pocket 3:** it focuses by itself, from 20 cm. Check its manual for manual focus.
- **Action 5 Pro:** fixed focus, sharp from about 35 cm to infinity. There is nothing to
  set. Just keep subjects further away than about 35 cm.

## Common mistakes

- **Judging focus at normal screen size.** Everything looks sharp on a small screen.
  Magnify before you trust it.
- **Trusting focus peaking alone.** It can glow on things that are only nearly sharp.
  Use it to get close, then check with magnification.
- **Turning to the end of the range for stars.** The end may be past infinity. Focus on
  a bright star with magnification instead.
- **Forgetting you are in MF.** The next scene comes out soft, because the camera won't
  refocus. Check the focus mode whenever you start a new scene.

## Try it

Twenty minutes on the L10, in the evening or in a dim room:

1. Find a plain wall with a small light switch on it. Try AF on the plain wall and note
   if it hunts.
2. Switch to MF. Focus on the switch once with magnification, and once with focus
   peaking only. Compare both frames at 100%.
3. Shoot through a window screen or a fence: first with AF, then in MF with the lens
   close to it.
4. Zoom to 24 mm, switch to AF Macro and focus on a coin about 5 cm away. Rock gently
   until the date is sharp.

Use the same magnify-and-check habit to place focus exactly in **A1.6**.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- Why does turning past sharp and back find focus faster than creeping up on it?
> You can only tell where the peak is once sharpness starts to fall again. Going past it
> shows you where it was. Contrast-detect autofocus uses the same trick.

> [!question]- At the zoo, autofocus keeps finding the wire fence instead of the bird. What do you do, and why does it work?
> Put the lens close to the fence and focus on the bird by hand. The wire is so far
> outside the depth of field that its blur spreads out and almost disappears.

> [!question]- Why not just turn the focus to the end of its range for stars?
> Many lenses can focus past infinity, so the end of the range can be soft. Magnify a
> bright star and turn until it is the smallest point.

> [!question]- Focus peaking outlines the whole face at f/1.7, but the eyes are soft in the photo. How?
> Peaking marks strong edges, and on a small screen it can't tell sharp from nearly
> sharp. It shows a range. Magnify the eye for the final check.

> [!question]- The Action 5 Pro has no focus control. When will its photos be soft because of focus?
> When the subject is closer than about 35 cm. Beyond that, everything is sharp.
