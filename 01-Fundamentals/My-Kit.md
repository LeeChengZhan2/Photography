## In one sentence
One real camera — the L10, with an iris, a viewfinder and a hot shoe — plus three
fixed-aperture, wide-angle, motion-first devices: Module 1 happens on the L10, and the
other three cover reach, water, motion and video.

## Lumix L10 — the camera the syllabus is written for

A premium compact from 2026: the Lumix GH7's 20.4 MP Four Thirds sensor behind a fixed
Leica 24–75 mm f/1.7–2.8 zoom, with an aperture ring, an eye-level viewfinder, an
[AF ON] button, a hot shoe and a mechanical shutter. It's the only device you own with
an iris and a viewfinder, so the aperture ladder, metering modes, back-button focus and
flash all happen here.

**Tips**
- **Fix the focal length before an aperture ladder.** The widest aperture shrinks as you
  zoom (f/1.7 at 24 mm, f/2.8 at 75 mm), so zooming mid-ladder changes two things.
- **Diffraction arrives early on Four Thirds.** With 3.3 µm pixels it starts to show
  between f/5.6 and f/8, and f/16 is the floor. Find where yours peaks in A1.1.
- **Under 50 cm, flip the focus selector to AF Macro.** Normal AF won't focus closer;
  AF Macro and MF reach 3 cm at 24 mm and 30 cm at 75 mm.
- **Choose the shutter on purpose.** Mechanical (to 1/2000) for flash and fast motion.
  Electronic (to 1/32000) for silence, and for f/1.7 in bright sun, which needs faster
  than 1/2000 at base ISO; there's no built-in ND. Electronic is a rolling shutter: fast
  motion leans, LED lights can band, and flash won't fire.
- **Back-button focus is built in.** Set [Shutter AF] to off and focus with [AF ON].
- **Aspect ratio isn't a crop.** The multi-aspect sensor makes 3:2 and 16:9 frames
  *wider* than 4:3 (5408 and 5664 px against 5200), so picking a ratio is a framing
  decision, not a loss.
- **For A1.5, turn [Auto Review] off and use the viewfinder.** It's the one device
  where "blind manual" works as written.
- **[REAL TIME LUT] (the [LUT] button) bakes a look into the JPEG/HEIF in camera.**
  Shoot RAW alongside while you're learning, so the look stays a decision. → [[LUTs]]
- **Live View Composite** adds only new bright areas during a long exposure: light
  trails without a blown-out scene, a ready-made "abstract" frame for A1.2.
- **Keep it dry.** It isn't splash-resistant; rain is the Action 5 Pro's job.

## iPhone 15 Pro Max — the one that's always with you

Three fixed-aperture lenses and a 48 MP main sensor, with Apple's computational
pipeline (multi-frame HDR, Night mode, Deep Fusion) shaping every file, ProRAW
included. Apple's own Camera app gives you exposure compensation and AE/AF lock but no
manual shutter or ISO. It's your reach (120 mm), your macro and the camera you'll
actually have on you.

**Tips**
- **Turn on ProRAW:** Settings → Camera → Formats → ProRAW & Resolution Control. Only
  the 1× lens saves 48 MP; 0.5×, 2×, 5×, Night mode, flash and macro shots save 12 MP.
- **Manual shutter and ISO need Halide or Lightroom**, and the shutter tops out around
  1 s. (Blackmagic Camera is video-only.) For A1.3's ISO ladder, shoot Halide's
  single-frame RAW rather than ProRAW, or you'll be measuring Apple's noise reduction.
- **Press and hold on the subject to lock focus and exposure (AE/AF LOCK)**, then drag
  the sun icon. That's your back-button focus and exposure compensation.
- **2× (48 mm) is a 12 MP crop of the main sensor at f/1.78**, good for portraits and
  A3.3. Past 5× it's digital zoom.
- **The ultra-wide has no OIS.** In dim light, brace yourself or switch to 1×.
- **Photographic Styles bake in at capture on this model** and can't be used with
  ProRAW. Leave Standard on if you plan to edit.
- **Video:** Apple Log needs ProRes. Internal ProRes stops at 4K30; 4K60 needs an
  external USB-C SSD. Apple's Log → Rec.709 LUT is on the Apple Developer site.

## DJI Pocket 3 — the walking video camera

A 1-inch sensor on a 3-axis mechanical gimbal, built for smooth handheld video and
vlogging. It takes stills, but only at about 9.4 MP (1:1) or 8.3 MP (16:9). It's the
best low-light *video* camera you own; for low-light *stills*, the L10's bigger sensor
wins.

**Tips**
- **Treat it as a video camera that also takes stills.** Stills come only in 1:1
  (3072 × 3072) and 16:9 (3840 × 2160); there's no 3:2 or 4:3.
- **Pro mode works in Photo mode too:** manual shutter (1/8000–1 s) and ISO
  (50–6400), enough for A1.2's slow end at dusk.
- **Rotate the screen** to switch between horizontal and vertical framing.
- **Gimbal modes:** Follow for walking, Tilt Locked to hold the pitch steady, FPV to
  roll with your movement.
- **For grading, shoot 10-bit D-Log M** and use DJI's Pocket 3 LUT, not the Action 5
  Pro's.
- **It focuses from 20 cm.** Closer than that, step back or use the iPhone.
- **Install the final firmware before 31 Dec 2026**, the date DJI's guaranteed software
  updates end.

## DJI Osmo Action 5 Pro — where nothing else can go

A 1/1.3-inch, 40 MP sensor behind a 155° fixed-focus lens, waterproof to 20 m without a
case, with electronic stabilisation for video. Its job is the places a camera
shouldn't go: water, mud, a bike mount, ankle height in traffic.

**Tips**
- **Choose the field of view:** Ultra Wide, Wide, or Standard (Dewarp). Standard
  (Dewarp) removes the fisheye bend, so use it for people and buildings.
- **After the L10 it has the longest shutter you own:** PRO mode photos go to 30 s and
  ISO 25600. Stills get no stabilisation, so mount it or use a tripod.
- **Focus is fixed from 35 cm to infinity.** Keep subjects beyond about 35 cm.
- **Waterproof to 20 m bare, 60 m in its case.** Rinse it in fresh water after the sea.
- **Stabilisation is video-only**, and HorizonSteady works only at 1080p or 2.7K 16:9,
  up to 60 fps.
- **For grading, shoot 10-bit D-Log M** and use the Action 5 Pro's own DJI LUT.
- **Use the front screen** to frame yourself.

## What this kit can and cannot do

1. **Only the L10 has an iris.** Aperture is a control on one device and a constant on
   the other three, so [[Aperture]] and A1.1 belong to the L10.
2. **Depth of field is deep on three devices and only moderately shallow on the L10.**
   On Four Thirds, f/1.7–2.8 blurs a background about as much as f/3.4–5.6 would on
   full frame. Separation still comes mostly from subject distance and background
   choice. → [[Depth-of-Field]] · [[Sensor-Size-and-Crop-Factor]]
3. **Focal length is well covered**, from 13 mm (iPhone 0.5×) to 120 mm (iPhone 5×),
   with the L10's 24–75 mm zoom in between. Perspective comes from distance, so a crop
   taken from the same spot compresses exactly like a longer lens.
4. **Only the L10 can use a real flash**, through its hot shoe and only with its
   mechanical shutter. Panasonic doesn't publish its flash sync speed, so finding it is
   a ready-made Module 2 experiment. The iPhone's flash is an LED.
5. **Only the L10 has a mechanical shutter and a viewfinder.** The other three are
   rolling-shutter, screen-only devices: fast motion leans, and you can't shoot them
   "blind".
6. **All four shoot log video** (V-Log, Apple Log, D-Log M twice), so [[LUTs]] are real
   work for you, and the L10 can apply one in camera, to stills too.
