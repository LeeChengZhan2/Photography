# Back-Button Focus

> [!summary] In one line
> Back-button focus moves autofocus off the shutter button and onto a separate button
> under your thumb. Focusing and taking the photo become two separate decisions.

## What it is

Out of the box, most cameras give one button two jobs. Half-press the shutter and the
camera focuses; press it fully and it takes the photo. Back-button focus splits the
jobs: a button on the back focuses (on the L10, **[AF ON]**), and the shutter button
only takes the photo.

## How it works

The camera focuses only while that button is held. Let go, and focus stays put. So:

1. **Focus once, recompose freely.** Press, let go, recompose, shoot. With the
   half-press method, each new half-press refocuses on whatever is under the AF point.
2. **Lock or track, without menus.** Leave the camera in AF-C. Hold the button and it
   tracks a moving subject. Let go and focus freezes, just like AF-S. One mode does
   both jobs, so you never switch modes mid-moment. → [[Autofocus-Modes]]
3. **The shutter never refocuses.** If someone walks between you and your subject, or
   you have pre-set a zone, pressing the shutter doesn't throw the focus away.

## How to use it

- **Still subject:** press [AF ON] once, let go, recompose, shoot.
- **Moving subject:** keep your thumb on [AF ON] while you shoot.
- **Zone focus for street:** press once on something at the right distance, then leave
  it alone. → [[Hyperfocal-Distance]]

**Who it suits:** anyone who shoots moving people or animals, recomposes a lot, or
pre-focuses for street. It also helps on a tripod, where focus must not change between
frames. **The downsides:** two buttons to coordinate, soft photos when you forget
[AF ON], and soft photos from anyone you hand the camera to.

## On your kit

- **L10:** set [Shutter AF] to off and focus with [AF ON]. Leave the focus mode on AF-C,
  so the one button can both track and hold.
- **iPhone 15 Pro Max:** press and hold on the subject for AE/AF LOCK. Focus and
  exposure stay put until you tap elsewhere, and the shutter only takes the photo. Drag
  the sun icon to adjust brightness. → [[Exposure-Compensation]]
- **Action 5 Pro:** focus is fixed, so there is nothing to separate.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting [AF ON].** The shutter won't focus. Make "thumb, then finger" one move.
- **Switching it on and off each outing.** Your thumb never learns. Keep it for a month.
- **Handing the camera over without warning.** Pre-focus on the spot first, or turn
  [Shutter AF] back on.
- **Thinking you need AF-S for still subjects.** AF-S works, but with [AF ON], AF-C
  does both jobs: hold to track, let go to lock. One mode means no switching mid-moment.

## Try it

Fifteen minutes on the L10, in AF-C with [Shutter AF] off, at f/4 or narrower:

1. Put a still subject 2 m or more away, off-centre. Press [AF ON] with the AF point on
   it, let go, recompose, and shoot three frames. Check that focus held.
2. Ask someone to walk around. Hold [AF ON] while they move, and shoot. Let go when they
   stop, and shoot again without refocusing.

Then use it for all twenty frames of **A1.6**, where placing focus exactly is the point.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- With back-button focus and AF-C, how do you get AF-S behaviour without opening a menu?
> Press [AF ON] and let go. Focus stops at the last distance and stays there until you
> press it again.

> [!question]- You pre-focused at 2.5 m for street shooting. Why does back-button focus protect that?
> The shutter never refocuses. Even if a car or a person passes close in front, the next
> press of the shutter keeps your 2.5 m setting.

> [!question]- What is the iPhone's nearest equivalent, and how is it different?
> Press and hold for AE/AF LOCK. Unlike [AF ON], it locks exposure too, and there is no
> "hold to track" option.
