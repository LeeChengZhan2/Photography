# Camera Menu Baseline

> [!summary] In one line
> A baseline is the set of settings you choose once, on purpose, and return to before
> every shoot. It stops leftover settings and factory defaults from quietly spoiling
> your photos, and it makes one week's results comparable with the next.

## What it is

Every camera arrives with its maker's defaults, and many don't suit learning: JPEG
only, image review on, the clock on the wrong time zone. A **menu baseline** is your
own default state for each device. You set it once with the manual open, write it down,
and return to it before each shoot.

It covers the settings you rarely touch. Aperture, shutter speed and ISO change from
shot to shot, so they aren't part of it.

## How it works

Each baseline setting prevents one kind of problem:

| Setting | What it prevents |
|---|---|
| Raw file format | Decisions baked in that you can't undo → [[RAW-vs-JPEG]] |
| Highest quality and size | Detail thrown away at capture |
| Formatting cards in the camera | Card errors, and old files mixed with new |
| Date, time and time zone | Files from four devices that won't sort together |
| Owner and copyright | Photos with no name attached (the camera writes it into each file) |
| Grid and level | Tilted horizons and careless placement |
| Live histogram | Exposure misjudged on a screen that looks too dark or too bright → [[Histogram]] |
| Image review | Checking every frame instead of watching the scene |
| Shutter type (L10 only) | Leaning motion, banding, or a flash that doesn't fire |
| Autofocus basics | Focus on the wrong thing, or lost on a moving subject → [[Autofocus-Modes]] |
| Silent operation | Noise where it isn't welcome |
| File numbering | Two files with the same name |

### Why the histogram beats the screen

A screen's brightness depends on its own setting and on the light around you: in
midday sun the same photo looks darker than it does indoors. The histogram is a graph
of the photo's tones, so it doesn't change with the screen. Judge exposure with it.

### Mechanical or electronic shutter

The L10's **mechanical** shutter uses physical curtains and reaches 1/2000 s. Its
**electronic** shutter starts and stops the exposure on the sensor itself, silently,
and reaches 1/32000 s. But it reads the sensor row by row, which takes time: fast
motion leans, LED lights can show bands, and a flash won't fire. So mechanical is the
default, and electronic is a choice you make for a reason. → [[Shutter-Speed]]

### Why file numbers repeat

Most cameras number files from 0001 to 9999 and then start again, and some restart on
a new card. With four devices, names soon clash. Continuous numbering, plus the date in
front of each name when you copy the files, keeps every name unique.
→ [[Ingest-and-Backup-Workflow]]

## How to use it

1. **Set each device once, with its manual open,** using "On your kit" below. Where it
   says "check the manual", the setting exists on most cameras, but its place in the
   menus varies.
2. **Write the baseline down** in [[My-Kit]]. If a camera can store settings as a
   custom mode, save the baseline there too.
3. **Reset before every shoot.** It takes 30 seconds: battery charged; card formatted
   (only after the last shoot is backed up); raw on; exposure compensation at 0; ISO,
   white balance and focus mode back to your defaults.
4. **Change the baseline on purpose, never by accident.** If an assignment needs a
   different setting, change it, then put it back afterwards.

## On your kit

**Lumix L10**
- **Files:** RAW (RW2) + JPEG or HEIF while learning, at the finest quality and the
  largest size.
- **Aspect ratio:** pick one, such as 3:2, and keep it through Module 1. The 3:2 and
  16:9 frames are wider than 4:3, not crops of it.
- **Look:** a plain Photo Style. Not V-Log yet: it starts at ISO 500 and gives a flat
  preview. Keep [REAL TIME LUT] off, because it bakes a look into the JPEG/HEIF.
  → [[LUTs]]
- **Shutter:** mechanical. Electronic for silence, or for speeds above 1/2000 s, such
  as f/1.7 in bright sun.
- **Focus:** single AF for still subjects, continuous AF for moving ones, and one small
  focus area that you place. Under 50 cm, flip the focus selector to AF Macro. For
  back-button focus, set [Shutter AF] to off and focus with [AF ON].
  → [[Back-Button-Focus]]
- **Review and display:** [Auto Review] off, which A1.5 requires. Grid, level and live
  histogram on; check the manual for where.
- **Housekeeping:** date, time and time zone; your name and copyright if the menu has
  them; continuous file numbering; beeps off. Format cards in the L10.

**iPhone 15 Pro Max**
- **Files:** turn on ProRAW in Settings → Camera → Formats → ProRAW & Resolution
  Control. Use it on the 1× lens when you'll edit: 48 MP there, 12 MP on the other
  lenses and in Night mode, flash and macro shots. HEIF is fine for everything else.
  Check that raw is switched on in the Camera app before an assignment.
- **Photographic Style:** Standard. On this model a Style bakes in at capture, and it
  can't be used with ProRAW.
- **Grid and level:** on, in the Camera settings.
- **Focus and exposure:** press and hold on the subject for AE/AF LOCK, then drag the
  sun icon to brighten or darken.
- **Manual shutter and ISO:** Halide or Lightroom only. Halide's single-frame RAW for
  A1.3.
- **Housekeeping:** the phone sets its own clock; check the time zone after travel.

**DJI Pocket 3**
- **Mode and files:** Photo mode with Pro mode on, for manual shutter (1/8000–1 s) and
  ISO (50–6400). Raw DNG. Stills come only in 1:1 (3072 × 3072) or 16:9
  (3840 × 2160).
- **Gimbal:** Follow by default. Tilt Locked holds the pitch steady; FPV rolls with you.
- **Focus:** from 20 cm. Closer than that, step back.
- **Firmware:** install the final version before 31 Dec 2026, when DJI's guaranteed
  updates end.

**DJI Osmo Action 5 Pro**
- **Mode and files:** PRO mode for photos (shutter to 30 s, ISO to 25600). Raw DNG.
- **Field of view:** Standard (Dewarp) for people and buildings; Wide or Ultra Wide when
  the fisheye bend doesn't matter.
- **Focus and stills:** focus is fixed, so keep subjects beyond about 35 cm. Stills get
  no stabilisation, so brace it or mount it.

On both DJI cameras, check the manual for the clock, grid, histogram, sounds and file
numbering. Only the L10 can be shot truly "blind". For A1.5 on the other three, don't
open the gallery or playback until you've finished.

## Common mistakes

- **Carrying yesterday's settings into today.** Exposure compensation left at +1, or
  ISO left at 6400, spoils a whole shoot. Do the 30-second reset.
- **Shooting JPEG only while learning.** You can't go back for the raw file later.
- **Using the L10's electronic shutter for everything.** Fast motion leans, LED lights
  band, and flash won't fire. Keep mechanical as the default.
- **Ignoring the clock.** One device on the wrong time scrambles every multi-device
  shoot. Check all four after travel or a flat battery.

## Try it

Thirty minutes, all four devices, manuals open:

1. Set each device's baseline from "On your kit", and shoot one frame on each.
2. Copy the files to your computer. Open each one's metadata (the shooting details
   saved inside the file) in your raw developer or photo viewer. Check the format,
   resolution, date and time.
3. Fix anything that surprised you, and write down the final baseline.

This completes the settings half of **A0.1** and prepares you for **A0.2**.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- Why does a wrong clock on one device matter, if the photos look fine?
> Every file is stamped with the time it was taken, and your software sorts by that
> stamp. A device on the wrong time puts its photos out of order, and one shoot looks
> like two.

> [!question]- The L10's electronic shutter is silent and faster. Why isn't it the default?
> It reads the sensor row by row. Fast motion leans, LED lights can band, and a flash
> won't fire. The mechanical shutter avoids all three, up to 1/2000 s.

> [!question]- Why judge exposure with the histogram rather than the screen?
> The screen's brightness changes with its setting and with the light around you. The
> histogram shows the photo's actual tones, so it reads the same in sun or shade.

> [!question]- A1.5 asks you to turn image review off. What do you gain?
> You stop checking and start predicting. With no picture after each shot, you have to
> judge the exposure from the scene and your settings, which is the skill being tested.
