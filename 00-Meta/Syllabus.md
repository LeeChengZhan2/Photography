# Photography Syllabus

A self-taught curriculum that goes from *"the camera decides"* to *"I decide, and I can
say why."* Seven modules, each with objectives, concepts to write notes on, practice
assignments, and an honest exit test.

> [!info] How to read this
> Every `[[Wikilink]]` below is a note you write, not a note you read. Clicking an
> unwritten link creates it — start from [[Concept-Note]] and fill it in your own
> words. Explaining a concept back is the actual learning; collecting links is not.

Companion documents: [[Roadmap]] (when) · [[Progress-Tracker]] (how far) ·
[[Glossary]] (terms) · [[Conventions]] (how this vault is organised)

---

## Guiding principles

1. **One variable at a time.** Every assignment isolates a single control. If you
   change aperture *and* light *and* subject, you learn nothing from the result.
2. **Shoot to a brief, not to a mood.** Constraints teach faster than freedom.
3. **Cull ruthlessly, and write down why.** The keeper rate matters less than being
   able to articulate the difference between the keeper and the reject.
4. **Theory follows failure.** Read the note *after* you've been confused in the
   field. It sticks.
5. **Manual first, automation later.** Learn what the camera is deciding before you
   let it decide again. Auto modes are a tool, not a starting point.

## Module 0 — Orientation & Setup

**Duration:** ~½ week · **Goal:** remove every friction that isn't learning.

### Objectives
- Know what your kit can and cannot do, in numbers.
- Have a repeatable ingest → backup → cull pipeline before the first real shoot.
- Understand why you're shooting RAW.

### Concepts
- [[My-Kit]] — bodies, lenses (focal range, max aperture), stabilisation, flash
- [[Camera-Menu-Baseline]] — the settings you set once and forget
- [[RAW-vs-JPEG]] — bit depth, latitude, why JPEG throws away decisions
- [[Ingest-and-Backup-Workflow]] — card → disk → 2nd copy, naming scheme
- [[Sensor-Size-and-Crop-Factor]] — equivalence, and what it does *not* change

### Assignments
- **A0.1 — Kit audit.** Write [[My-Kit]] from the manual/specs, not memory. Record
  each lens's focal range, widest aperture, minimum focus distance.
- **A0.2 — Dry run.** Shoot 20 throwaway frames. Ingest, back up, cull, export one
  JPEG. Time it. Fix whatever was annoying.

### Exit test
You can find any setting on your camera without the menu-diving hesitation, and your
backup has two copies on two devices.

## Module 1 — Exposure & the Camera as a Machine

**Duration:** 4 weeks · **Goal:** full manual control, deliberately chosen, every time.

This is the module people skip and then plateau on for years. Don't.

### Objectives
- Predict the exposure change from any setting change, in stops, before you shoot.
- Choose aperture/shutter/ISO from *creative intent*, not from what looks correct.
- Read a histogram and know if the file has the information you need.

### Concepts
- [[Exposure-Triangle]] — the trade space, not a rule
- [[Stops-and-EV]] — the one bit of arithmetic that matters; halving and doubling
- [[Aperture]] — f-number, light vs depth of field, diffraction, sweet spot
- [[Shutter-Speed]] — freeze vs blur, reciprocal rule, handheld limits, panning
- [[ISO]] — gain not sensitivity, noise floor, dual-native ISO, ISO-invariance
- [[Metering-Modes]] — evaluative/matrix, centre-weighted, spot; when each lies
- [[Exposure-Compensation]] — what it actually does in A/S vs M
- [[Histogram]] — reading tonal distribution, clipping, why "correct" is a choice
- [[Dynamic-Range]] — highlight vs shadow latitude, [[Expose-to-the-Right]]
- [[Shooting-Modes]] — P / A / S / M / Auto-ISO, and which pros actually use
- [[Depth-of-Field]] — aperture × distance × focal length, [[Hyperfocal-Distance]]
- [[Autofocus-Modes]] — single vs continuous, area modes, subject detection
- [[Back-Button-Focus]] — decoupling focus from shutter
- [[White-Balance]] — colour temperature, tint, custom WB, why RAW forgives it

### Assignments
- **A1.1 — The aperture ladder.** One static subject with clear foreground,
  midground and background. Shoot every full stop from wide open to f/16. Keep
  exposure constant by compensating with shutter. Note where sharpness peaks and
  where diffraction starts costing you.
- **A1.2 — The shutter ladder.** One moving subject (traffic, a tap, a walking
  friend). 1/1000 → 1s. Find the *three* speeds that read as "frozen",
  "suggested motion", and "abstract".
- **A1.3 — The ISO ladder.** Same frame at base ISO through max. Push shadows +2
  stops in post on each. Decide your personal *usable* ceiling — the number you'll
  trust in the dark.
- **A1.4 — Meter defeat.** Photograph a white wall and a black jacket in the same
  light on evaluative metering. Explain why both came out grey and fix each with
  exposure compensation.
- **A1.5 — Blind manual.** 50 frames, full manual, no chimping (screen off / tape
  it). Then review. Your hit rate is your grade.
- **A1.6 — DOF prediction.** Before pressing the shutter, write down what will be in
  focus. 20 frames. Score yourself.

### Exit test
Someone names a scene and an intent ("moving kid, indoors, want them sharp and the
background soft") and you state settings out loud, correctly, in under five seconds.

## Module 2 — Light

**Duration:** 4 weeks · **Goal:** see light before you see subject.

### Objectives
- Describe any light in four words: quality, direction, colour, contrast ratio.
- Find good light rather than fixing bad light.
- Shape light with one modifier and one flash.

### Concepts
- [[Quality-of-Light]] — hard vs soft, and why *relative source size* is the only
  thing that controls it
- [[Inverse-Square-Law]] — fall-off, and using distance as a contrast control
- [[Direction-of-Light]] — front, 45°, side, back, rim, top, under
- [[Colour-Temperature-of-Light]] — golden hour, blue hour, shade, mixed sources
- [[Contrast-Ratio]] — lighting ratios and how to meter them
- [[Natural-Light]] — window light, open shade, overcast, harsh midday
- [[Light-Modifiers]] — reflector, diffuser, scrim, flag, bounce surface
- [[Flash-Fundamentals]] — guide number, TTL vs manual, [[Sync-Speed]], HSS
- [[Bounce-Flash]] — ceilings, walls, and never pointing it straight ahead
- [[Off-Camera-Flash]] — one light, triggers, feathering
- [[Balancing-Flash-and-Ambient]] — shutter for ambient, aperture/power for flash
- [[Available-Light-Discipline]] — when *not* to add light

### Assignments
- **A2.1 — One window, one subject, eight positions.** Move the subject around a
  single window. Same camera settings where possible. Label each frame with quality
  and direction. This single exercise teaches more than any book chapter.
- **A2.2 — Hard-to-soft.** Bare bulb / phone torch, then diffuse it progressively
  (paper, curtain, wall bounce). Document the shadow edge at each step.
- **A2.3 — Fall-off.** Subject 0.5 m from a window, then 1 m, then 2 m. Show the
  inverse-square law with actual frames and exposure readings.
- **A2.4 — Same subject, four times of day.** Sunrise, midday, golden hour, blue
  hour. Identical framing. Write which one wins and why.
- **A2.5 — Backlight rescue.** Backlit portrait, three solutions: expose for face,
  reflector fill, flash fill. Compare.
- **A2.6 — One light only.** Flash off-camera, key light only, no fill. Five
  portraits, five different feels.

### Exit test
Walk into an unfamiliar room and, within a minute, point to where you'd put a person
and why.

## Module 3 — Composition & Visual Language

**Duration:** 4 weeks · **Goal:** deliberate frames. Every element present on purpose.

### Objectives
- Say what a frame is *about* in one sentence, and defend every inclusion.
- Use focal length as a perspective choice, not a zoom convenience.
- Subtract.

### Concepts
- [[Subject-and-Intent]] — what is this photograph *of*, and what is it *about*
- [[Focal-Length-and-Perspective]] — compression vs expansion; perspective is set
  by *subject distance*, not by the lens
- [[Frame-and-Aspect-Ratio]] — 3:2, 4:3, 1:1, 16:9, and orientation as a decision
- [[Rule-of-Thirds]] — the training wheel, and when to take it off
- [[Symmetry-and-Centring]] — when the middle is the right answer
- [[Leading-Lines]] · [[Diagonals-and-Dynamism]]
- [[Visual-Weight-and-Balance]]
- [[Negative-Space]] — space as subject
- [[Layers-and-Depth]] — foreground / midground / background, near-far
- [[Framing-Devices]]
- [[Pattern-Repetition-and-the-Break]]
- [[Colour-Theory-for-Photographers]] — harmony, complementaries, colour as subject
- [[Tonal-Composition]] — notan, luminance masses, where the eye lands
- [[Simplification]] — the subtractive habit
- [[Gesture-and-Moment]] — the decisive moment, layering timing onto composition
- [[Juxtaposition]]

### Assignments
- **A3.1 — Fifty frames, one subject.** One object or one street corner. Fifty
  genuinely different frames. Frames 1–15 are obvious, 16–35 are work, 36–50 are
  where you learn.
- **A3.2 — Prime jail.** One focal length for a full week. If you only have a zoom,
  tape it. This is the highest-leverage exercise in the whole syllabus.
- **A3.3 — Compression vs expansion.** Same subject filling the frame at your widest
  and longest focal length — move your feet to match subject size. Compare backgrounds.
- **A3.4 — Subtract.** Take five of your own busy photographs. Reshoot each with one
  element removed. Then another. Stop when it breaks.
- **A3.5 — Three layers.** Ten frames, each with a clear foreground, midground and
  background that all contribute.
- **A3.6 — Monochrome colour study.** Ten frames where a single colour is the subject.
- **A3.7 — Squint test.** Convert 20 of your frames to blurred greyscale. Does the
  composition still read? Those that don't were relying on detail.

### Exit test
Show ten frames. For each, state the subject, the intent, and one thing you removed.

## Module 4 — Post-Processing & Colour

**Duration:** 4 weeks · **Goal:** finish a file to your intent, repeatably.

Tool-agnostic — the concepts transfer between Lightroom, Capture One, Darktable and
RawTherapee. Pick one and stop shopping.

### Objectives
- Run a full ingest → cull → develop → export → archive cycle without thinking.
- Edit toward a decision made at capture, not toward whatever the sliders suggest.
- Produce a consistent look across a set of twelve images.

### Concepts
- [[Editing-Workflow]] — the five stages and their order
- [[Culling-Discipline]] — flags/ratings, one pass, no second-guessing
- [[Non-Destructive-Editing]] — catalogues, sidecars, virtual copies
- [[Tone-Curve]] — contrast, black point, filmic shoulders
- [[Basic-Panel-Order]] — WB → exposure → contrast → highlights/shadows → presence
- [[HSL-and-Colour-Mixer]] — targeted hue/saturation/luminance
- [[Local-Adjustments]] — masks, gradients, radials, subject/sky detection
- [[Dodge-and-Burn]] — directing the eye with luminance
- [[Sharpening]] — capture vs creative vs output sharpening
- [[Noise-Reduction]] — luminance vs colour, and AI denoise
- [[Lens-Corrections]] — distortion, vignette, CA
- [[Colour-Grading]] — shadows/midtones/highlights, split toning
- [[Black-and-White-Conversion]] — channel mixing, not desaturating
- [[Colour-Management]] — [[Colour-Spaces]] (sRGB / AdobeRGB / ProPhoto), monitor
  calibration, soft proofing
- [[Export-Settings]] — web vs print vs archive
- [[Developing-a-Look]] — presets as a starting point, never an ending point
- [[Archive-Strategy]] — 3-2-1, catalogue backups, folder taxonomy

### Assignments
- **A4.1 — One file, three interpretations.** Same RAW → bright and airy, moody and
  contrasty, black and white. Write which serves the subject.
- **A4.2 — Recovery limits.** Bracket one scene from −3 to +3 EV. Push each back to
  normal. Find where your sensor's data actually runs out.
- **A4.3 — Cull under pressure.** 100 frames → 10 keepers in 15 minutes. Then, a day
  later, re-cull cold. Compare the two sets.
- **A4.4 — Dodge and burn only.** Edit a frame using nothing but local luminance.
- **A4.5 — Set of twelve.** One consistent look across twelve images from different
  sessions. This is much harder than it sounds and is the real skill.
- **A4.6 — Print one.** Actually print it, A4 or larger. Note everything the screen
  lied about.

### Exit test
Hand yourself a folder of 200 frames and produce a finished, coherent set of twelve
in under two hours.

## Module 5 — Genre Deep-Dive

**Duration:** 4 weeks · **Goal:** depth in two genres, and the vocabulary of the rest.

Pick **two**. Write a note for each covering: what makes a good one, the technical
constraints, the standard settings starting point, the ethics, the practitioners to
study, and one project idea.

- [[Street-Photography]] — anticipation, layers, legality and ethics, zone focusing
- [[Portraiture]] — direction, posing, lens choice, rapport, eye light
- [[Landscape]] — planning, filters, focus stacking, hyperfocal, weather
- [[Architecture]] — verticals, perspective correction, tilt-shift, timing
- [[Night-and-Astrophotography]] — 500 rule, star trails, light painting, stacking
- [[Macro]] — magnification, working distance, DOF collapse, diffusion, stacking
- [[Wildlife-and-Birds]] — reach, AF tracking, fieldcraft, ethics
- [[Still-Life-and-Product]] — full control, tabletop lighting, compositing
- [[Documentary-and-Photojournalism]] — narrative arc, consent, captioning
- [[Sports-and-Action]] — pre-focus, burst discipline, peak action
- [[Travel-Photography]] — research, sense of place, avoiding the postcard

### Assignments
- **A5.1 — Study before shoot.** For each chosen genre, analyse 20 images by
  photographers you admire in [[Critique]] notes before shooting any of your own.
- **A5.2 — Ten-frame set.** A cohesive ten-frame set in each chosen genre.
- **A5.3 — Constraint shoot.** Shoot one chosen genre with the "wrong" gear
  (landscape on a long lens, portrait on a wide). Learn what the convention buys.

### Exit test
Your ten-frame set is recognisable as that genre to someone who wasn't told.

## Module 6 — Seeing, Critique & a Body of Work

**Duration:** 4 weeks · **Goal:** stop making photographs and start making work.

### Objectives
- Critique a photograph with specific language instead of "I like it".
- Sequence images so the set says more than the sum of the frames.
- State your own intent in writing.

### Concepts
- [[Reading-a-Photograph]] — describe → analyse → interpret → judge, in that order
- [[Critique-Vocabulary]] — the words that replace "nice"
- [[Photographers-to-Study]] — a running list with *what specifically* to steal
- [[History-of-Photography-Skim]] — pictorialism → f/64 → decisive moment → New
  Colour → New Topographics → contemporary
- [[Editing-a-Sequence]] — pairing, rhythm, opening and closing frames
- [[Series-vs-Singles]]
- [[Personal-Style]] — what recurs when you're not trying
- [[Artist-Statement]] — 150 words, no jargon
- [[Portfolio-Construction]]
- [[Sharing-and-Feedback]] — where to post to get criticism rather than likes

### Assignments
- **A6.1 — Twenty critiques.** Twenty photographs by others, four-step method, one
  [[Critique]] note each. Half should be photographs you *dislike*.
- **A6.2 — Self-critique cold.** Critique your own Module 1 output with the same
  rigour. Be specific about what improved.
- **A6.3 — Steal deliberately.** Reproduce three images by a photographer you admire.
  Then make a fourth that's yours.
- **A6.4 — Sequence.** Take 30 of your keepers and sequence 12 of them. Justify the
  first and last.

### Exit test
Your artist statement and your portfolio agree with each other.

## Capstone

**Duration:** 4 weeks (overlaps Module 6).

A **cohesive series of 12–20 finished images** on a single subject or idea, plus:
- a 150-word [[Artist-Statement]],
- a shoot plan written *before* you started,
- one print,
- a short retrospective: what you'd do differently.

Deliberately narrow the subject. "My neighbourhood at 6 a.m." beats "street
photography". A tight brief is what makes a body of work read as one thing.

---

## Assessment

There are no grades. There are four honest questions, asked at each module boundary in
[[Progress-Tracker]]:

1. Can I explain this to someone else without notes?
2. Can I do it under time pressure in the field?
3. Can I point at frames in my own library that prove it?
4. What did I get wrong, and did I write it down?
