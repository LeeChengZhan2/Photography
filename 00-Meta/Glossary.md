# Glossary

Terms in one line each. Where a term has its own note, the entry links to it — open the
note for the full explanation. When a new term confuses you, add it here.

## A–C

- **Aperture** — the lens opening, in f-numbers. Smaller number = bigger hole = more
  light, less depth of field. → [[Aperture]]
- **B-roll** — extra footage that shows what a film is about, cut in over the main
  shots. → [[Shot-Sizes-and-Coverage]]
- **Banding** — visible steps in what should be a smooth gradient, such as a sky,
  usually after heavy edits to a low-bit-depth file. → [[Bit-Depth]]
- **Bit depth** — how many levels each colour channel can store: 8-bit has 256, 10-bit
  has 1,024, raw files 4,096 or more. → [[Bit-Depth]]
- **Bitrate** — how much data a video stores per second. More data keeps more detail.
  → [[Codecs-and-Bitrate]]
- **Bokeh** — the *character* of out-of-focus rendering, not the amount of it.
  → [[Depth-of-Field]]
- **Bracketing** — shooting the same frame at several exposures. → [[Photo-Merges]]
- **Chromatic aberration** — colour fringing because a lens bends each wavelength
  differently. *Axial* CA focuses them at different distances; *lateral* CA magnifies
  them differently, fringing towards the corners (what "Remove CA" fixes).
  → [[Lens-Corrections]]
- **Clipping** — tonal values pushed past pure black or pure white; the data is gone.
  → [[Histogram]]
- **Codec** — the method used to compress video, such as H.264, H.265 or ProRes.
  → [[Codecs-and-Bitrate]]
- **Colour space** — the range of colours a file or screen can hold: sRGB, Display P3,
  Rec.709 and others. → [[Colour-Spaces]]
- **Colour temperature** — the warmth/coolness of light in kelvin. Confusingly,
  *higher* K = cooler/bluer. → [[White-Balance]] · [[Colour-Temperature-of-Light]]
- **Coverage** — enough different shots of a scene that it can be edited together.
  → [[Shot-Sizes-and-Coverage]]
- **CPL (circular polariser)** — a filter that cuts reflections and darkens blue sky.
  → [[Lens-Filters]]
- **CRI** — a score out of 100 for how faithfully a light shows colours; about 95 or
  higher is good. → [[Continuous-Light]]
- **Crop factor** — the multiplier relating a sensor's field of view to full-frame.
  → [[Sensor-Size-and-Crop-Factor]]
- **Culling** — choosing the photos to keep and rejecting the rest.
  → [[Culling-Discipline]]

## D–F

- **Depth of field** — the zone that reads as acceptably sharp. → [[Depth-of-Field]]
- **Diffraction** — resolution loss at small apertures. Where it starts depends on pixel
  size: about f/11 on 24 MP full frame, f/5.6–f/8 on 20 MP Four Thirds. → [[Aperture]]
- **Dodge and burn** — brightening (dodge) and darkening (burn) parts of a photo to
  guide the eye. → [[Dodge-and-Burn]]
- **Dynamic range** — the span from darkest to brightest the sensor can record.
  → [[Dynamic-Range]]
- **EV (exposure value)** — one number for an aperture + shutter pair: EV 0 is f/1 at
  1 s, and each step up lets in a stop less light. It's an absolute scale; a
  *difference* of 1 EV is one stop. → [[Stops-and-EV]]
- **ETTR** — expose to the right: give a raw file as much light as the picture allows
  without clipping important highlights, then darken it in editing.
  → [[Expose-to-the-Right]]
- **Fall-off** — how fast light dims with distance. → [[Inverse-Square-Law]]
- **Flat lighting** — light from the camera's position; minimal shadow, minimal form.
  → [[Direction-of-Light]]
- **Focal length** — in mm; determines field of view on a given sensor.
  → [[Focal-Length-and-Perspective]]
- **Focus peaking** — coloured outlines on the screen that show what's in focus.
  → [[Manual-Focus]]
- **Frame rate (fps)** — how many pictures per second a video records, e.g. 25 or 50.
  → [[Frame-Rate]]

## G–L

- **Gamut** — the full range of colours a colour space or screen can show.
  → [[Colour-Spaces]]
- **Golden hour / blue hour** — the warm, low light around sunrise and sunset, and the
  deep blue light just before sunrise and after sunset. Both are short near the
  equator. → [[Natural-Light]]
- **Guide number** — a flash's power rating: distance × f-number for a correct exposure,
  quoted at ISO 100 and a stated zoom-head setting. → [[Flash-Fundamentals]]
- **HDR** — either merging several exposures into one photo with more dynamic range
  (→ [[Photo-Merges]]), or video and screens with a wider brightness range than
  standard SDR (→ [[Video-Export-Settings]]).
- **HSS (high-speed sync)** — pulsing flash to work above [[Sync-Speed]], at a
  power cost.
- **Hyperfocal distance** — focus point that maximises near-to-infinity sharpness.
  → [[Hyperfocal-Distance]]
- **Inverse-square law** — double the distance, quarter the light.
  → [[Inverse-Square-Law]]
- **ISO** — how brightly the camera renders a given exposure. At native settings it's
  mostly analogue gain applied during readout, before digitisation; it doesn't make the
  sensor more sensitive to light. → [[ISO]]
- **J-cut / L-cut** — a cut where the next shot's sound starts early (J), or the last
  shot's sound carries on over the new picture (L). → [[Cuts-and-Transitions]]
- **Jump cut** — a cut between two very similar shots, so the subject seems to jump.
  → [[Cuts-and-Transitions]]
- **Key light** — the dominant light shaping the subject. → [[Contrast-Ratio]]
- **Lighting ratio** — lit side against shadow side. Two conventions: (key + fill) : fill,
  where equal lights give 2:1, or key : fill. Say which one you use. → [[Contrast-Ratio]]
- **Log (log profile)** — a capture curve that stores dynamic range compressed and flat,
  expecting a later transform: a technical [[LUTs|LUT]] or a colour-space transform.
  → [[Log-Profiles]]
- **Long exposure** — a shutter speed long enough to show movement as blur or trails,
  from about 1/15 s to minutes. → [[Long-Exposure]]
- **LUT (look-up table)** — a stored input-colour → output-colour map. 1D = three
  per-channel curves; 3D = a lattice over the whole RGB cube, so it can do cross-channel
  moves a curve can't. → [[LUTs]]

## M–R

- **Mask** — a selection that limits an edit to one part of the photo.
  → [[Local-Adjustments]]
- **Metering** — measuring the light to suggest an exposure. Whatever the meter reads,
  it aims to make middle grey. → [[Metering-Modes]]
- **ND filter (neutral density)** — darkened glass that cuts light without changing its
  colour. → [[Lens-Filters]] · [[ND-Filters]]
- **Noise** — random speckles caused by too little light; high ISO makes it visible.
  → [[Noise-Reduction]]
- **Non-destructive editing** — edits saved as instructions, so the original file never
  changes. → [[Non-Destructive-Editing]]
- **Notan** — the light/dark mass structure of an image, ignoring detail.
  → [[Tonal-Composition]]
- **Panning** — tracking a moving subject at slow shutter for a sharp subject and
  streaked background. → [[Shutter-Speed]]
- **PPI (pixels per inch)** — how densely a print's pixels are packed; about 300 for
  prints seen up close. → [[Printing]]
- **Proxy** — a small copy of a video clip that edits smoothly and is swapped back for
  the original at export. → [[Video-Editing-Workflow]]
- **RAW** — minimally processed sensor data plus metadata. Phone raw can be more than
  that: Apple ProRAW is already demosaiced and multi-frame. → [[RAW-vs-JPEG]]
- **Reciprocity** — trading one exposure control against another to hold brightness.
  → [[Stops-and-EV]]
- **Rim light** — a light behind the subject that outlines its edge.
  → [[Direction-of-Light]]
- **Rolling shutter** — reading the sensor row by row rather than all at once, so fast
  motion leans and flickering lights band. Phones and action cameras use one for every
  shot. → [[Shutter-Speed]]

## S–Z

- **Screen direction (the 180° line)** — keep the camera on one side of the line
  between two people, or along a movement, so directions stay consistent from shot to
  shot. → [[Screen-Direction]]
- **Shutter angle (the 180° rule)** — in video, a shutter speed of about 1/(2 × frame
  rate), e.g. 1/50 s at 25 fps. → [[Shutter-Angle]]
- **Soft light** — light from a source that is *large relative to the subject*.
  Distance matters as much as physical size. → [[Quality-of-Light]]
- **Soft proofing** — previewing on screen how a photo will look when printed.
  → [[Colour-Management]] · [[Printing]]
- **Stop** — a doubling or halving of light. The unit everything is measured in.
  → [[Stops-and-EV]]
- **Sync speed** — the fastest shutter that fully exposes with flash.
  → [[Sync-Speed]]
- **Tetrahedral interpolation** — the better way to guess values between a 3D
  [[LUTs|LUT]]'s nodes: more accurate than trilinear, and it keeps greys neutral.
- **Time-lapse** — frames taken at intervals and played back at normal speed, so time
  runs faster. → [[Time-Lapse-and-Hyperlapse]]
- **Timeline** — the strip in a video editor where clips are laid out in order.
  → [[Timeline-and-Trimming]]
- **Tone curve** — the input→output brightness mapping. Mechanically a 1D LUT.
  → [[Tone-Curve]]
- **TTL** — through-the-lens automatic flash metering. → [[Flash-Fundamentals]]
- **Vignetting** — corner darkening, optical or added for effect. → [[Lens-Corrections]]
- **Waveform** — a graph of brightness across the frame, used to expose video.
  → [[Zebras-and-Waveform]]
- **White balance** — neutralising (or deliberately shifting) a light's colour cast.
  → [[White-Balance]]
- **Zebras** — moving stripes on the screen over areas brighter than a level you set.
  → [[Zebras-and-Waveform]]
