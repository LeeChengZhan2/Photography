# Shoot Planning

> [!summary] In one line
> Deciding before you go what you want to make, where, when, with what, and what you'll
> do if it goes wrong. A plan turns a hopeful walk into a shoot you can judge later.

## What it is

A shoot plan is a short written note made before a shoot. It answers eight questions:

| Part | The question |
|---|---|
| Brief and intent | What am I making, and what is it about? |
| Location | Where exactly will I stand, and what is behind the subject? |
| Timing | When is the light right in that place? |
| Weather | What will the sky do, and what if it rains? |
| Shot list | Which frames must I come back with? |
| Gear | What do I need, charged and with empty cards? |
| Permissions | Do I need anyone's agreement, for the place or the people? |
| Plan B | What do I do if the plan fails? |

You'll use it for the weekly assignments, for the **A5.6** one-minute film (which needs
a shot list written before you film), and for the capstone, which asks for a shoot plan
written before you start.

## How it works

A plan works in three ways.

**It turns hope into a prediction.** "Get something good at the market" can't fail, so
it can't teach you anything. "Six frames of hands at work, in side light from the open
shutters, before 9 a.m." can. Afterwards you compare the plan with the result in your
[[Shoot-Log]], and the gap is the lesson.

**It moves the hard decisions to where you have time.** On location you're busy with
the camera, the light and the people. At home you have time to work out the sun's
direction or a backup location.

**It frees your attention on the day.** With the must-have frames written down, you can
stop worrying and look.

### Light in Singapore

Singapore is about 1° north of the equator, so the sun behaves differently from what
most photography books describe.

- **Sunrise is around 7:00 and sunset around 19:00, all year.** The sun is highest
  around 13:00, so the harshest light runs from late morning to mid-afternoon.
- **The warm light is short.** The sun rises and sets steeply, so low, warm light lasts
  only about 20–30 minutes after sunrise and before sunset. Blue hour is shorter still.
  Be set up early. → [[Colour-Temperature-of-Light]]
- **The sun's direction shifts through the year.** From about March to September it
  rises and sets north of due east and west, up to about 23° in June; from September to
  March, south. A wall that catches the evening sun in May or June may get none in
  December.
- **Midday light comes from almost straight above.** Shadows fall into eye sockets and
  under hats. Covered walkways, void decks and the shade of tall blocks give soft open
  shade instead. → [[Natural-Light]]

Sun-position apps such as PhotoPills or Sun Surveyor, listed in [[Resources]], show
where the sun will be from any spot, at any time and date.

### Weather

Heavy thunderstorms are common, often in the afternoon or evening, and they build fast.
Check the forecast the day before and a rain radar on the morning. Haze, when it comes,
flattens contrast and mutes colour. If you hear thunder, get under proper shelter: an
open field, a beach or a rooftop is the wrong place to be.

## How to use it

### The steps

1. **Write the brief and the intent,** one sentence each. → [[Subject-and-Intent]]
2. **Scout.** If you can, visit at the time you plan to shoot. Note where the light
   comes from, where you'd stand, what is behind the subject, how crowded it gets, and
   where you can shelter. Take a quick phone photo from each spot.
3. **Fix the time** with a sun app, and keep a reserve slot later in the week.
4. **Write the shot list.** Must-haves first, then nice-to-haves. For a series: an
   opener, a closer, a wide, details, people. For the A5.6 film: shot sizes, cutaways
   and sound. → [[Shot-Sizes-and-Coverage]]
5. **Pack and reset the gear the night before.** Charge the batteries. Back up the
   cards, then format them. Reset ISO, exposure compensation, white balance and file
   format from the last shoot. → [[Camera-Menu-Baseline]]
6. **Sort out permissions.** Ask the management of private places, and ask the people
   you'll feature. → [[Copyright-and-Consent]]
7. **Write plan B:** a covered location nearby, a reserve time, a wet-weather subject
   (reflections, umbrellas, rain on glass), and a second device.

Cold gear from air-conditioning fogs up in humid air. Take the camera out of the cold
early, sealed in its bag, and let it warm up before you open the bag.

### After the shoot: the retrospective

The capstone asks for a short retrospective, and one helps after any big shoot. Within
a week, write half a page that answers four questions:

1. **What did I plan, and what actually happened?** Put the plan next to the results.
2. **What worked, and why?** Name the frames, and the decision behind each.
3. **What failed, and was it technique, understanding or conditions?**
4. **What will I do differently next time?** One or two concrete changes.

Keep it honest and specific, like a good [[Shoot-Log]]. It is the bridge from one
project to the next.

### A template to copy

```markdown
## Shoot plan: [title]
- **Brief:** what I'm making (e.g. 10 frames, a one-minute film)
- **Intent:** what it's about, in one sentence
- **Location:** [place] · **Plan B location:** [covered, nearby]
- **Date and time:** [date], [start–end] · sunrise/sunset: [times]
- **Light:** where the sun is at that time; which side of the subject it lights
- **Weather:** forecast the day before / radar on the day / if it rains: [plan]
- **Must-have shots:** 1. … 2. … 3. …
- **Nice-to-have shots:**
- **Gear:** [devices] · charged batteries · empty cards · cloth · rain cover · water
- **Starting settings:** mode, aperture, shutter, ISO, white balance, file format
- **Permissions:** place [ ] · people [ ]
- **Risks and plan B:**
- **Afterwards:** back up, then write the shoot log
```

## On your kit

- **Lumix L10:** it isn't splash-resistant. On a stormy day, carry a cover (a plastic
  bag works), or make the Action 5 Pro, waterproof to 20 m, your wet-weather camera.
- **Pocket 3:** stills come only in 1:1 or 16:9. If your series is 3:2 or 4:3, plan
  those frames on the L10 or the iPhone.
- **For A5.6:** the iPhone, Pocket 3 and Action 5 Pro have fixed apertures. Without an
  ND filter, they can't hold 1/50 s in daylight. Nor can the L10 in full sun: even at
  f/16, its smallest aperture and deep into diffraction, it's about a stop too bright.
  Plan scenes at dusk, indoors or in shade, or accept a faster shutter. → [[ND-Filters]]

## Common mistakes

- **Planning the place but not the time.** The same street is a different picture at
  8:00 and at 13:00. Check the sun before you go.
- **A shot list with no must-haves.** If everything is optional, you'll come home
  without the frame the set needed. Mark three as essential.
- **No plan B.** Storms are common here. Know your covered alternative before you
  leave.
- **Yesterday's settings.** ISO 6400 and −2 EV from last night's shoot will spoil a
  bright morning. Reset the night before.
- **Treating the plan as a cage.** If something better appears, shoot it, then go back
  to the list.

## Try it

Thirty minutes at home, for your next real shoot.

1. Fill in the template above.
2. In a sun app, check where the sun will be at your location at two possible times.
   Choose one, and write down why.
3. Write a six-shot list with three must-haves.
4. After the shoot, tick what you got, and write one line on each miss.

This is practice for the capstone's shoot plan in week 29, and for the shot list in
**A5.6**.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- Why write a plan before you shoot, when it will probably change?
> It turns a vague hope into a prediction you can check. Afterwards you compare the plan
> with the result and learn from the difference. Changing the plan on the day is fine;
> having nothing to compare against isn't.

> [!question]- In late May you want low, warm light on the west-facing side of an HDB block. When do you go, and what do you check?
> The last half hour before sunset, roughly 18:30–19:00. In May the sun sets well north
> of due west, so check in a sun app that its light actually reaches that wall, and
> that the next block doesn't shade it.

> [!question]- Thunderstorms are forecast from 15:00 on your shoot day. What are your options?
> Move the shoot to the morning, switch to your covered plan B location, or make the
> rain the subject with the Action 5 Pro. The reserve slot later in the week is the
> last resort.

> [!question]- What does a shot list for the A5.6 film need that a stills shot list doesn't?
> Coverage: wide, medium and close shots of the same action so you can cut between
> them, plus cutaways, the movement for each shot, and sound such as room tone. You can
> only edit the shots you took.
