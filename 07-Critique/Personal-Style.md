# Personal Style

> [!summary] In one line
> Your style is what recurs in your photographs when you're not trying: the subjects,
> distances, light and moments you keep choosing. You find it by shooting a lot and
> looking back honestly, not by picking a preset.

## What it is

Style is the pattern in your choices. Look at a hundred of your photographs together and
some things will repeat. You stand far back, or very close. You love backlight. You
centre things. You wait for empty streets. You like muted colour.

Some of those repeats come from what you care about. Some come from habit, or from fear.
Your style is the part you would defend: the repeats that serve what you want to say.

Style is **not** a preset, a filter or a gimmick. Those are a finish, added at the end.
Style starts much earlier, when you decide what to point the camera at.

## How it works

### Style has layers

| Layer | Examples | Easy to copy? |
|---|---|---|
| What you care about | People at work, quiet places, humour, family | No. It comes from your life |
| How you approach it | Close or far, eye level or low, wait or chase | Hard |
| How you use light and space | Backlight, open shade, negative space, centring | Medium |
| How you finish | Palette, contrast, grain, crop | Easy. A preset copies it |

A preset copies only the bottom row. That's why two people using the same preset still
make different work, and why a preset alone never makes a style. → [[Developing-a-Look]]

### Where the pattern comes from

- **Your interests.** You keep noticing the same kinds of things.
- **Your limits.** Where you live, when you're free to shoot, and your kit all leave a
  mark. A fixed 20 mm lens, or shooting only in the evening after work, shapes the
  pictures.
- **Your temperament.** Patient people wait for the moment. Restless people move and
  find new angles.
- **Your influences.** Everything you've looked at feeds in.

### Volume and reflection

You need both. Volume without reflection is just habit: you repeat yourself without
knowing it. Reflection without volume has nothing to work on: twenty frames show no
pattern. Hundreds of keepers, looked at honestly, do.

### Influence vs copying

| Copying | Influence |
|---|---|
| Takes the whole picture: subject, framing, light, edit | Takes one decision and uses it on your own subjects |
| One source | Many sources, mixed |
| Stops at the copy | Moves on to a frame that's yours |

Copying is a good way to learn, which is why **A7.3** asks you to reproduce three
images. The problem is showing a copy as your own style. Steal the decision, not the
picture.

## How to use it

Run a style audit once, near the end of Module 7.

1. **Gather your 100 best frames** from the whole course. Use your ratings and flags.
   → [[Culling-Discipline]]
2. **See them all at once:** a grid on a big screen, or printed contact sheets.
3. **Count.** Most raw developers can filter by focal length and aperture. Tally the
   rest by hand: distance to the subject, light, colour or black and white, people or
   no people, orientation.
4. **Write down three things that recur, and one thing you avoid.**
5. **Sort each one: choice or habit?** A choice is something you can defend: "I centre
   because the symmetry calms the scene." A habit is not: "I always shoot at 24 mm
   because I never zoom." Fear counts as habit: "I shoot people from behind because I'm
   shy."
6. **Push one choice further** on purpose for a week. **Test one habit** by doing the
   opposite once.

Then hold your audit up against the photographers you keep going back to on
[[Photographers-to-Study]]. What did you take from them, and what is only yours?

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing a style before you have the work.** You can't find a pattern in pictures
  you haven't made. Shoot first, look back later.
- **Calling a preset a style.** A preset is a finish. Two people with the same preset
  still make different work.
- **Changing your look every week.** Then nothing can recur. Stay with one way of
  working long enough to see it.
- **Hiding weak photos under a heavy edit.** A strong look on a weak frame is still a
  weak frame.
- **Avoiding all influence for fear of copying.** Influence is how everyone starts. Mix
  many sources, and name them in your notes.
- **Thinking style means one subject forever.** Style can travel across subjects. It is
  how you see, not only what you see.

## Try it

Thirty minutes with your last 50 keepers.

1. Make a tally table with six columns: focal length, distance (close / medium / far),
   light (front / side / back / flat), people (yes / no), colour or black and white,
   orientation.
2. Tick each frame into the table.
3. Circle the biggest number in each column. For each, write one sentence: choice or
   habit?

This feeds your cold self-critique in **A7.2** and the [[Artist-Statement]] you draft in
week 28.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- Why can't you choose a style first and then shoot it?
> Style is a pattern across many real decisions. Before you've made the pictures, there
> is nothing to find. A chosen look without the concerns underneath is only a surface.

> [!question]- Your audit shows 80% of your frames at 24 mm. Is that your style?
> Not yet. Ask why. If 24 mm suits how close you like to work, it may be a real choice.
> If you simply never zoom, it's a habit, and worth testing with a week at 75 mm.

> [!question]- What is the difference between influence and copying?
> Copying takes the whole picture. Influence takes one decision, such as waiting for a
> subject to enter a composed frame, and uses it on your own subjects, mixed with ideas
> from other sources.

> [!question]- A friend says your photos "all look the same". Is that good or bad?
> It depends on what is the same. A shared way of seeing, across varied subjects, is a
> style. The same subject, framing and moment again and again is repetition. Ask them
> which they mean.
