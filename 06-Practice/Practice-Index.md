# Practice

Output, not theory. Everything in here is evidence — the record of what you actually
did, which is what makes the concept notes in the other folders trustworthy.

## Subfolders

| Folder | What goes in it | Naming |
|---|---|---|
| `Shoot-Logs/` | One entry per shoot, no exceptions | `YYYY-MM-DD-slug.md` |
| `Critiques/` | Other people's photographs, four-step method | `Critique-Photographer-Subject.md` |
| `Daily/` | Daily notes (Obsidian creates these automatically) | `YYYY-MM-DD.md` |

Templates: [[Shoot-Log]] · [[Critique]] · [[Assignment]] · [[Daily-Practice-Log]]

## The two rules

1. **The log gets written the same day.** A shoot without a log is a shoot you'll learn
   nothing from — by next week you won't remember which frame used which setting or why
   the light went wrong.
2. **Every log links the concepts it tested.** Those backlinks are how you later answer
   "show me my own frames that prove I understand depth of field." Without them the
   concept notes are just repeated theory.

## What good looks like

A useful log is specific about failure. "Lighting was off" teaches nothing. "Key light
was 2 m away so fall-off was too even — should have moved it to 0.8 m and dropped power
two stops" is something you can act on next time. → [[Inverse-Square-Law]]

## Reviewing

Once a month, read the last four weeks of logs in one sitting and ask:

- What failure repeats? That's the next concept note to rewrite.
- What time of day or location never works? Design around it, don't keep retrying.
- Which concepts have no logs linking to them? Those are things you've read, not learnt.

Then update [[Progress-Tracker]] honestly, and bump `confidence` in the concept notes
that earned it.
