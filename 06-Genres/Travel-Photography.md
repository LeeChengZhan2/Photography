# Travel Photography

> [!summary] In one line
> Photographs that show what a place is like to be in: its light, people, food and
> everyday life, not just its landmarks. The work is research before you go, attention
> while you're there, and respect for the people who live there.

## What makes a good one

- **A sense of place.** The picture could only have been taken here: the light, the
  materials, the script on the signs, the food, the way people greet each other.
- **People living their lives,** not only monuments.
- **A set that tells the trip:** arrival, places, people, details, an ending. Not 600
  random frames. → [[Editing-a-Sequence]]
- **No postcards,** unless you use one knowingly.

## The hard part

### Avoiding the postcard shot

A postcard is the landmark, from the famous viewpoint, in good light, usually without
people. Everyone already has it, so it tells no one anything new.

- **Take it once,** quickly, if you want the record. Then look for your own picture.
- **Turn around.** Photograph the crowd photographing the landmark.
- **Walk five streets away.** Show the landmark small, behind daily life.
- **Come back at an odd hour:** dawn, rain, closing time.
- **Find the details and the routines:** doors, tiles, hands, signs; the market at
  dawn, the morning commute, the evening walk.

A useful test: cover the landmark with your thumb. Does the picture still say where you
are? → [[Subject-and-Intent]]

### Sense of place

What makes a place itself is often small and everyday: the colour of the light at that
latitude, the building materials, the script on signs, school uniforms, the way people
queue. Shoot a mix of distances, as a documentary photographer would: wide frames for
the setting, medium frames for people in it, close frames for details. Look for old and
new side by side. → [[Juxtaposition]]

### Research

Good travel pictures start at home.

- **Look first.** Study photographers who have worked there (A6.1), so you know the
  clichés and can avoid them.
- **Plan the light and the life.** Check sunrise, sunset and the season: monsoon, dry
  season, winter? Note market days, festivals, opening hours and prayer times.
  → [[Shoot-Planning]]
- **Learn the rules.** Military sites, borders and airports are off-limits in many
  countries. Some countries restrict drones, and some give people strong rights over
  their own image (France is a well-known example). Some religious sites ban cameras.
- **Learn a few words,** including "May I take your photograph?"

### Photographing people, and asking permission abroad

- **Ask.** Smile, gesture to the camera, use your few words. A "no" is an answer: thank
  them and move on.
- **Customs differ.** In some places people expect payment for a portrait; decide your
  own rule, and be consistent and respectful. In others, photographing women, children
  or ceremonies is sensitive.
- **Children:** ask the parent, and respect a no.
- **Give back.** Show them the picture on the screen. Offer to send it, and actually
  send it.
- **Be more careful abroad, not less.** You're a guest, and you may misread a situation
  that a local would understand at once.

### Packing light

A heavy bag makes you leave the camera at the hotel. Take one main camera and the phone.

- **L10 and iPhone** cover 13–120 mm between them. Add the Action 5 Pro for water and
  beaches; take the Pocket 3 only if you will film.
- **Spare batteries** go in your cabin bag: airlines don't allow spare lithium batteries
  in checked luggage.
- **Back up every night.** Copy the cards to a laptop, drive or phone, and keep the copy
  in a different bag from the camera. → [[Ingest-and-Backup-Workflow]]
- **Weather:** the L10 isn't splash-resistant, so carry a plastic or dry bag. In cold
  places, batteries drain faster. Moving from cold into warm, humid air fogs the lens;
  let the camera warm up in a closed bag first.

## Settings to start with

- **L10:** A mode at f/4–f/5.6, Auto ISO capped at your A1.3 ceiling. 24–35 mm for
  places, 75 mm for details and portraits. Zone focus in busy streets. RAW.
- **A consistent look:** the L10's Real Time LUT bakes one look into the JPEG while the
  RAW keeps your options open. → [[LUTs]]
- **iPhone:** quick and discreet. 0.5× for interiors where photography is allowed, 5×
  for details across a square, Night mode for evening streets.
- **Action 5 Pro** for boats, beaches, rain and snorkelling; **Pocket 3** for smooth
  walking video in Follow mode.

## Gear reality

Forums say you need a bag of lenses, a big tripod and a drone. You need the camera you
will carry all day, every day. The L10 and the iPhone already cover more focal lengths
than most travellers use. → [[My-Kit]] Every extra kilo is a cost, and theft is easier
when you carry more than you can watch.

## Ethics and the law

- **You're a guest, and local law applies to you.** Rules on government buildings,
  military sites, transport hubs and people's images vary. Check before you go. Laws
  change; this is general guidance, not legal advice. → [[Copyright-and-Consent]]
- **Religious sites:** follow the dress code, use no flash, and don't photograph people
  at prayer unless it is clearly allowed.
- **Poverty is not scenery.** Ask whether the picture respects the person in it.
- **Paid or posed portraits** are fine if your caption says so.
- **Location tags:** sharing the exact spot of a fragile place can bring crowds that
  damage it.

## Who to study

- **Robert Frank** — *The Americans* (1958): a Swiss photographer's road trip across the
  US in the mid-1950s. Look at how jukeboxes, cars, flags and diners describe a country
  better than any monument: an outsider's eye on ordinary life.
- **Harry Gruyaert** — colour work in Morocco over many years: saturated walls, hard
  light and deep shadow, small figures. Look at how blocks of colour build a sense of
  place.
- **Martin Parr** — *Small World*: tourists at famous sights around the world, shot with
  flash in bright colour. Look at how he photographs the tourism, not the sight: the
  most direct lesson in avoiding the postcard.

## A project you can finish in a week

**Tourist at home.** Choose a Singapore neighbourhood you don't know well, such as Joo
Chiat, Tiong Bahru, Geylang Serai, Balestier or Kampong Glam, and treat it like a
foreign city. Do the research first: its history, one food stall, opening times, the
sunset time. Visit twice, one morning and one evening. Ten frames, with one rule: no
landmark fills more than a quarter of any frame. Include one portrait taken with
permission, one food frame, one sign and one wide view. Would a visitor learn what the
place feels like? That's your A6.2 set.

## Check yourself

> [!question]- What makes a picture a "postcard", and what are two ways to use the same landmark without making one?
> The famous view, in good light, with no life in it: everyone already has it. Instead,
> turn around and photograph the people photographing it, or show it small behind daily
> life from a side street.

> [!question]- Why does research before a trip improve even the pictures you didn't plan?
> You already know the light, the timing and the customs, so you waste less time and
> make fewer mistakes. Your attention is free to notice what is specific to the place.

> [!question]- Someone abroad shakes their head as you raise the camera. You could still take it from further away. Why don't you?
> They said no. Legal or not, taking it disrespects them, and you're a guest. In some
> countries it may also break laws that protect people's image.

> [!question]- Why pack the Action 5 Pro rather than the Pocket 3 for a beach trip?
> The Action 5 Pro is waterproof to 20 m. The Pocket 3 has no water rating, and the L10
> isn't splash-resistant either.
