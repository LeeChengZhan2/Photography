# Photographers to Study

A starting list, not a canon. The column that matters is **what to steal** — a specific,
repeatable decision. "Great atmosphere" teaches nothing; "shoots through foreground
obstructions to build layers" is something you can try on Saturday.

Write a [[Critique]] note before you decide you like someone's work. Verify the
attributions and go to primary sources — monographs and gallery sites beat aggregator
blogs.

## Structure & the moment

| Photographer | Known for | What to steal |
|---|---|---|
| Henri Cartier-Bresson | Geometry + timing, 50 mm Leica, mid-20th c. | Waiting on a composed frame for the subject to enter it |
| Elliott Erwitt | Wit and visual coincidence | Humour as a compositional device |
| Fan Ho | 1950s–60s Hong Kong, graphic light | Shafts of light as structure, not decoration |
| Josef Koudelka | High-contrast B&W, exiles, gypsies | Dramatic tonal masses; [[Tonal-Composition]] |
| Vivian Maier | Street, self-portraits, TLR | Waist-level viewpoint changes everything |

## Colour

| Photographer | Known for | What to steal |
|---|---|---|
| Saul Leiter | Colour street through glass, snow, reflections | Obstruction as foreground layer; long lens on the street |
| William Eggleston | Elevated the banal; "democratic camera" | Colour as subject; nothing is too ordinary |
| Alex Webb | Dense, layered colour, Magnum | Multiple simultaneous events in one frame |
| Joel Meyerowitz | Colour street, then large-format light studies | Same location, repeatedly, across light |
| Ernst Haas | Early colour pioneer | Motion blur used as colour |
| Harry Gruyaert | Saturated, cinematic ambiguity | Colour blocks as composition |

## Light & tone

| Photographer | Known for | What to steal |
|---|---|---|
| Ansel Adams | Zone system, US landscape | Previsualising the print before the exposure |
| Irving Penn | Studio portraits and still life | Radical simplification; one light, one idea |
| Richard Avedon | Portraits on white, *In the American West* | Removing all context to force attention |
| Michael Kenna | Minimalist long-exposure B&W landscape | Time as a tool for simplification |
| Rinko Kawauchi | Soft, high-key, quiet Japanese photobooks | Delicate light; sequencing over single frames |
| Todd Hido | Suburban night, houses, weather | Mood built from available light alone |

|---|---|---|

## How to use this list

1. Pick **one** photographer per week. One is enough.
2. Find 20 images from a single body of work, not a "best of" scrape. Series reveal
   intent; greatest-hits reveal nothing.
3. Write one [[Critique]] using the four-step method.
4. Reproduce one image as an exercise (assignment **A6.3**), then make a fourth frame
   that's yours.
