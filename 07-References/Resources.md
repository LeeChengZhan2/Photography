# Resources

Curated, not exhaustive. Collecting resources is a very comfortable way to avoid
shooting — the rule is **one book and one channel at a time**.

## Books — technique

| Book | Author | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| *Understanding Exposure* | Bryan Peterson | Module 1. The clearest exposure-triangle explanation in print. |
| *Light: Science and Magic* | Hunter, Biver, Fuqua, Reid | Module 2. The definitive book on **why** light behaves as it does. Dense; worth it. |
| *The Photographer's Eye* | Michael Freeman | Module 3. Composition treated as design rather than rules. Not Szarkowski's 1966 MoMA book of the same name, which suits Module 7. |
| *In the Blink of an Eye* | Walter Murch | Module 5. A great film editor on why and where to cut. Short; the classic. |
| *Grammar of the Edit* | Roy Thompson & Christopher J. Bowen | Module 5. The working vocabulary of shots, cuts and continuity. |
| *The Camera* / *The Negative* / *The Print* | Ansel Adams | Deep dive. Still the best writing on previsualisation. |
| *Read This If You Want to Take Great Photographs* | Henry Carroll | Fast, visual, good for momentum in week 1. |

## Books — seeing

| Book | Author | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| *The Nature of Photographs* | Stephen Shore | Module 7. A short primer on how to *read* a photograph. Start here. |
| *Ways of Seeing* | John Berger | How images carry meaning. Not about cameras at all. |
| *On Photography* | Susan Sontag | The ethics and philosophy. Argumentative; disagree with it. |
| *Camera Lucida* | Roland Barthes | Difficult, rewarding, on why particular photographs wound you. |
| *Bystander: A History of Street Photography* | Westerbeck & Meyerowitz | If you pick street in Module 6. |

Also: buy or borrow **monographs** — a single photographer's sequenced body of work.
For Module 7 a photobook teaches more than any tutorial, because sequencing is the
lesson.

## Free technical references

- **Cambridge in Colour** — tutorials on optics, DOF, diffraction, colour management.
  The best free technical writing on the web.
- **Strobist "Lighting 101"** — the classic free off-camera-flash course. Do this
  alongside Module 2, week 8.
- **Your camera's actual manual** — genuinely. Module 0 is much faster with it open.

## Video

Pick one. Watching is not practising.

- **Sean Tucker** — seeing, intent, the philosophy of a personal voice. Best pairing
  with Module 7.
- **The Photographic Eye (Alex Kilbee)** — critique and history; how to look at work.
- **The Art of Photography (Ted Forbes)** — photographer profiles, art-history angle.
- **Thomas Heaton / Nigel Danson** — landscape, decision-making shown in the field.
- **Simon d'Entremont** — clear technical explanation, wildlife-leaning.
- **Every Frame a Painting (Tony Zhou & Taylor Ramos)** — film language and editing,
  one idea per essay. It ended in 2017; the essays still hold. Best pairing with
  Module 5.

## Software

Pick **one** RAW developer and stay with it for the whole syllabus. Switching tools
mid-way costs you the muscle memory that Module 4 is meant to build.

| Tool | Notes |
|---|---|
| Lightroom Classic / Lightroom | Subscription. Best ecosystem, excellent AI denoise and masking. |
| Capture One | Perpetual or subscription. Superb colour and tethering. |
| Darktable | Free, open source, scene-referred workflow. Steep learning curve. |
| RawTherapee / ART | Free. Very strong detail rendering. ART is the friendlier fork. |
| DxO PhotoLab | Best-in-class optical corrections and denoise. |
| Affinity (by Canva) | Free with a Canva account since Oct 2025. Photoshop-style layers, including LUT adjustments. |

### Video editors — Module 5

Same rule: pick **one** editor and stay with it.

| Tool | Notes |
|---|---|
| DaVinci Resolve | Free; the paid Studio version adds more. Windows, Mac, Linux and iPad. Editing, colour and sound in one app, and it already reads your V-Log, Apple Log and D-Log M footage and their LUTs. Blackmagic's free official training is the place to start. |
| CapCut | Free, with a paid tier. Phone and desktop. The fastest route to a short social clip, with templates and auto-captions; less control over colour and sound than Resolve. |
| LumaFusion | One-time purchase. iPhone and iPad. A real multi-track editor on a device you already carry; reads `.cube` LUTs. |
| Premiere Pro | Subscription. Windows and Mac. Widely used in industry; pairs with Lightroom if you chose Adobe for Module 4. |
| Final Cut Pro | Mac and iPad only — not on Windows. |

## Planning & field tools

- **PhotoPills** or **Sun Surveyor** — sun/moon position, golden hour, planning by AR.
- **Windy** / local met service — cloud cover is the single most useful forecast.
- **Clear Outside** / **Light Pollution Map** — if you pick astro in Module 6.

## Places to get real criticism

Likes are not feedback. You want somewhere people will tell you the frame is boring.

- A local camera club or a photowalk group — in-person critique is the highest bandwidth.
- Curated platforms (LensCulture, Aperture) for reading, and open calls for deadlines.
- One trusted person who shoots better than you and will be blunt. This is worth more
  than every platform combined.

## Gear — deliberately last

You do not need to buy anything to complete Modules 0–5. The L10 covers aperture,
shutter, ISO and metering, and the other three cover reach, water and motion. A3.2
needs no prime either: the Pocket 3 is a fixed 20 mm, or pick one L10 focal length and
leave it. Module 5 edits in free software. The only purchases the syllabus genuinely
benefits from, in order:

1. A **manual speedlight** for the L10's hot shoe — Module 2, week 8. The L10 has no
   built-in flash and the other three can't sync one. The Roadmap's continuous-light
   substitute works if you'd rather not buy.
2. A **tripod** — the L10's 60 s and the Action 5 Pro's 30 s exposures, and Module 4's
   bracketing.
3. A **reflector** (or a piece of white foam board, for free).
4. **ND filters** for the device you film on most — Module 5. They keep the 180°
   shutter usable in daylight; dusk, indoors and open shade work without them.

Anything else, write it in a wish list and revisit after the capstone. Kit notes live in
[[My-Kit]].
